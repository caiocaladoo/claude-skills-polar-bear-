# Conversation Opener

## When To Use
They accepted the connection, and the next message is where most people pitch. Or they replied to a comment of yours and the thread stopped at "thanks". This answers: what is the honest reason to write to this person now, and how do I make it easy for them to say yes or no?

## When Not To Use
Not for people who have not accepted or answered anything: use Connection Request Note, or wait. Not for a string of follow-ups. If no reason is true yet, the right move is not to write.

## Inputs
- The exchange so far, pasted: your request note, their acceptance or reply, the comment thread.
- How well you know them, in your own words. For most people in this guide the honest answer is "barely" or "not at all".
- What you would like to come of it.
If you have none of this, I start from the person's name and one line on what happened, and mark the note as a first draft.

## Approach
One reason that serves the reader, with an easy way to decline, described generically. Accepting a request is a small yes to being connected and nothing more, so the ask is sized to that: a share needs no ask, and a conversation is offered, never assumed. Nothing here is sent or automated on LinkedIn; you type the note yourself, in line with the platform's published rules on automation. The failure it prevents: a new connection turned cold by a pitch in the first private message.

## Workflow
1. Ask at most three questions: what happened in the exchange, how well you know them, and what you would like to come of it.
2. Pick one reason with you: share something useful, congratulate, offer an introduction, or suggest a conversation. If none is true yet, the output says "do not write yet".
3. Pull two or three points from the real exchange only: what they said, what you said, the open question. Each point names where it came from.
4. Size the ask to how little you know each other. For someone you barely know: a share with no ask, or one question about their work. A call is suggested only if they opened that door, with a topic and a short length you set.
5. Add the easy way to decline in plain words, such as "no need to reply if the timing is wrong".
6. Check the plan: no pitch in a first note, no flattery, nothing implied that did not happen, no link to your services.
7. You write the note and send it yourself. One note. If there is no answer, it rests; there is no chasing sequence.

## Output Format
```markdown
# Conversation Opener Note
To: [name] | How well you know them: [your words] | The exchange: [one line, with date and where]
## Reason to write
[share / congratulate / introduce / suggest a conversation]: [why it is useful to them], or "do not write yet"
## Points from the real exchange
1. [point] (from: [their reply / your note / the thread])
2. [point] (from: [source])
3. [optional point] (from: [source])
## The ask and the easy no
- Ask: [none / one question / a topic and a short time]
- Easy no: [plain line, in your words]
## Decision
[Your name] writes and sends the note by [date], or decides not to write yet.
```

## Done When
- One reason is chosen, and it is useful to the other person.
- Every point traces to the real exchange.
- The ask matches how little you know each other.
- The plan carries an easy way to decline and no pitch.

## Quality Bar
- An accepted request is not interest in your services, and is never treated as such.
- Introductions only after both people have agreed.
- No follow-up sequences, no chasing, no template sent to many.
- Plain words, short, in your voice.
- You write and send the note; Claude prepares the points and nothing is automated on LinkedIn.
