# Cold Call Outline

## When To Use
You avoid the phone because you do not know what to say after hello. This answers: what do I say, in what order, and how do I respond to the two refusals I hear most, without reading from a page?

## When Not To Use
For a first contact in writing, use Cold Email Outline. For a call the other person has agreed to, use First Call Prep: there you have time for real questions, here two are enough.

## Inputs
- The person you are calling (name, role, organisation) and your reason, with the fact it rests on.
- What you want from the call: usually a short conversation at a time that suits them.
- Two times you can offer.
If you have none of this, I start from the role and your reason in one sentence, and mark the outline as a first draft.

## Approach
A permission-based opening, then the reason in one line: generic sales practice, with no author behind it. The person was not expecting you, so asking whether the moment suits is the only honest way to begin, and it stops being honest the day it becomes a trick. You get keywords, not sentences, because a script read aloud sounds read. Rules on calling people you do not know differ by country and by number type: check with a qualified adviser. The failure it prevents: a long recited monologue that ends in "send me something".

## Workflow
1. Ask at most three questions: who you are calling, your true reason, and what you will ask for at the end.
2. Opening: who you are, in a few words, then "is this a good moment?". If it is not, you ask when would be better and end the call. That is a successful opening.
3. The reason for the call in one line, from the fact you have. One line means one breath.
4. Two open questions about their situation. You note what they say in their words; the answers are for the conversation you are asking for, not for a pitch now.
5. The ask: a short conversation, with your two times. One ask.
6. Two common refusals. "Send me something" and "we already have someone": for each, acknowledge it, ask one question to understand, offer one light next step. If the answer is still no, accept it and thank them.
7. Voicemail of about twenty seconds: your name, the reason, how to reach you. No pressure and no invented urgency. I place no call and dial no number.

## Output Format
```markdown
# Cold Call Outline
Calling: [name, role, organisation] | Reason: [one line, fact and source] | Goal: [a short conversation]
## The call, in keywords
| Moment | Keywords (say it your way) |
|---|---|
| Opening | [who I am] · [is this a good moment?] · [if not: when is better] |
| Reason | [one line] |
| Questions | [open question 1] · [open question 2] |
| Ask | [short conversation] · [time 1] · [time 2] |
## Common refusals
| What you hear | Acknowledge | One question to understand | Light next step |
|---|---|---|---|
| "Send me something" | [keywords] | [keywords] | [keywords] |
| "We already have someone" | [keywords] | [keywords] | [keywords] |
## Voicemail (about twenty seconds)
[name] · [reason] · [how to reach you]
## Decision
[Your name] decides who to call and when, and what to note after each call, by [date].
```

## Done When
- The opening asks permission and covers the case where the moment is wrong.
- The reason fits in one line and rests on a true fact.
- Each refusal has a question and a light next step, and a no is accepted.
- Every cell holds keywords, not sentences.

## Quality Bar
- Keywords only: you speak the way you speak.
- No manipulation, no false urgency, no trick questions.
- A no is recorded as a no, and a request not to be called again is honoured at once.
- Legal points on calling are questions for you: check with a qualified adviser.
- An outline to say in your own words; you make the call, and Claude places none.
