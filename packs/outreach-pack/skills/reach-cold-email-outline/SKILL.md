# Cold Email Outline

## When To Use
You must write to someone who does not know you, and you refuse to send spam. You have a reason and your points. This answers: in what order do I say it, how small should the ask be, and how do I make it easy to say no or stop?

## When Not To Use
If someone you know could introduce you, ask them instead: a warm path beats the best cold email. If there is no real reason to write yet, do not write; keep the account on the Buying Signals Watchlist. This skill gives a frame, never a draft.

## Inputs
- The person, the one reason you chose, and your Talking Points.
- Your Warm Path Check result for this account.
- Optional: the Gmail connector, used to search and read for a past thread with this person. It never creates a draft and never sends.
- Your Writing Voice Guide, if you have one.
If you have none of this, I start from the reason in one sentence and the fact it rests on, and mark the outline as a first draft. With no real reason, I stop.

## Approach
One person, one real trigger, the reason in the first sentence: a plain writing habit, described generically. The reader decides in the first line whether this is for them, so the reason goes there and everything about you waits. Because the Gmail connector can also draft and send, it is used here to search and read only. Rules on unsolicited email differ by country: check with a qualified adviser. The failure it prevents: "I hope you are well. My name is..." followed by a paragraph about you, deleted before the second line.

## Workflow
1. Ask at most three questions: the reason you chose, what you would like them to be able to answer in one line, and whether I may search your inbox for a past thread.
2. Check before anything else. A warm path exists: stop and ask for the introduction. A past thread exists: this is not cold, and the opening must say so. No sourced reason: stop.
3. Lay out the frame as five empty slots: a plain subject that says what the email is about; the reason in the first sentence; one or two sentences of substance from your points; one question that is easy to answer and easy to decline; a plain way to say stop.
4. You type your own words into each slot. I do not fill a slot, suggest a phrase or offer a "version to edit".
5. Flag by line once you have written: a subject that baits, an opening about you, more than one ask, a fact with no source, flattery, false familiarity, a line from the AI-tell checklist in your voice guide. Flags name the problem and stop there.
6. Set one follow-up date and what it would add that is new. If it adds nothing, there is no follow-up. After one, the thread closes.
7. One email to one person. No variants for other names, no sequence, no scheduled send.

## Output Format
```markdown
# Cold Email Outline
To: [name], [role] | Warm path: [none found / ask for an introduction] | Past thread: [none / date] | Reason: [one line, fact number]
## Frame (your words in every slot)
| Slot | What belongs here | Your words |
|---|---|---|
| Subject | Plain, says what the email is about | [you write] |
| First sentence | The reason you are writing | [you write] |
| Substance | One or two sentences from your points | [you write] |
| Question | One, easy to answer, easy to decline | [you write] |
| A way to say stop | One plain line | [you write] |
## Flags
| Line | Flag | Why |
|---|---|---|
| [slot or line] | [bait / about you first / second ask / no source / flattery / false familiarity] | [one sentence] |
## Follow-up
[Date you set]: [the new thing it adds], or "none". After that, the thread closes.
## Decision
[Your name] rewrites the flagged lines and decides whether to send, and sends it personally by [date]; or waits for a stronger reason.
```

## Done When
- The warm path and past thread were checked first.
- Every slot holds your own words; none was filled for you.
- Every flag names a line and a reason, with no replacement wording.
- At most one follow-up is planned, with something new in it.

## Quality Bar
- One email to one person; no sequences, no mail merge, no reuse across names.
- Public work facts only; nothing personal, nothing implying you know them.
- The ask fits in one reply, and no is an easy answer.
- Legal points on unsolicited email are questions for the Outreach Rules Check: check with a qualified adviser.
- Claude gives the frame and the flags; you write every word and press send, and nothing is drafted in your mailbox.
