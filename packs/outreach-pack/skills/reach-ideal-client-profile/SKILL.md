# Ideal Client Profile

## When To Use
You say yes to anyone, and when you sit down to do outreach you cannot say who a first message should go to. This answers one question before any name is chosen: in what situation does someone need exactly you, and what tells you early that it will go wrong?

## When Not To Use
If you have fewer than three finished engagements, the profile is a guess: write it, mark it as one, and come back after the next few. If you already know who you are for and need the moments that would make them need you this month, go to Buying Situations Map.

## Inputs
- Three to five of your best past engagements and two or three of your worst, in your own words: what had just happened when they called, what you did, how it went. No client names needed.
- Anything a client said about why they hired you, from your notes or emails, and one engagement you declined or lost, for the test at the end.
If you have none of this, I start from two engagements you remember well, one good and one bad, and mark the output as a first draft.

## Approach
Jobs to Be Done, as the Christensen Institute describes it on its theory page: people do not buy a service, they hire it to make progress in a particular circumstance. So the profile describes situations and the progress wanted, never a type of organisation. The failure it prevents is the profile written from imagination: a client you wish you had, which then points your outreach at people who have never needed you.

## Workflow
1. Ask at most three questions: which engagements count as best and worst by your own reasons, what you want more of in the next year, and which client you would never take again and why.
2. For each best engagement, find the circumstance: what had just happened when they called (a change, a deadline, a failure, a new person in a role). Write it as a trigger someone outside could notice, then keep the two or three that repeat.
3. Write the job in three dimensions, from what clients said and did: functional (the work that had to get done), emotional (how the buyer wanted to feel), social (how they wanted to be seen, and by whom). Quote your notes where you have them; anything I infer is marked "my reading, to check".
4. Map the roles in the decision as you observed them: who felt the problem, who signed, who could block, who used the work. Roles only, never a judgement of the people in them.
5. Turn what went wrong in the worst engagements into red flags you could notice on a first call, such as "nobody owns the budget". You mark each one walk away or ask more.
6. Test the draft against your best engagement and the one you declined or lost. If it would have screened out a client you loved, it is wrong, and we fix it together.
7. Finish with the line outreach needs: "a first message goes to [role] when [trigger]". If you cannot fill it, the profile is not done.

## Output Format
```markdown
# Ideal Client Profile
Drawn from: [number] best and [number] worst engagements, described by [your name]
## Situation and trigger
| Trigger someone outside could notice | Seen in | What waiting costs them |
|---|---|---|
| [what had just happened] | [engagement A, engagement C] | [in their words, or "unknown"] |
## The job they hire you for
- Functional: [the work that has to get done]
- Emotional: [how the buyer wants to feel]
- Social: [how they want to be seen, and by whom]
## Roles in the decision
| Role in the decision | What they cared about | Seen in |
|---|---|---|
| [feels the problem / signs / can block / uses the work] | [from your notes] | [engagement] |
## Red flags
| Signal you can observe on a first call | Seen in | Walk away or ask more |
|---|---|---|
| [observable signal] | [engagement] | [your call] |
## Who a first message goes to
A first message goes to [role] when [trigger].
## Decision
[Your name] decides which red flags are walk-away rules and tests the profile on the next [number] conversations, by [date].
```

## Done When
- Every trigger, job line and red flag traces back to a past engagement of yours.
- The profile describes situations and roles, never a size, sector or type of organisation.
- Each red flag is something you could observe on a first call.
- The "who a first message goes to" line is filled in.

## Quality Bar
- Situations, not demographics: "a new leader inherited a stalled project" beats any label.
- Inferences are marked "my reading, to check"; nothing is presented as what a client said unless they said it.
- Roles describe a part in a decision, never a person's character or worth.
- Built from your real past work, never an imagined client; you choose who to reach.
