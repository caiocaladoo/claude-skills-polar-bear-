# Buying Situations Map

## When To Use
You know who you are for and not what would make them need you this month. So you write to people at a random moment and hope. This answers which moments start a need for your work, and what someone outside the organisation could see when one of them happens.

## When Not To Use
If you have not yet written down who hired you and why, run Ideal Client Profile first; the map is built on it. If you already have accounts and want to know what to watch at each one, that is the Buying Signals Watchlist.

## Inputs
- Your Ideal Client Profile, or notes on your best past engagements.
- How each past engagement started, as far as you remember: what had happened, who called whom.
- Optional: situations you suspect matter but have never worked on.
If you have none of this, I start from three engagements you remember and mark the output as a first draft.

## Approach
Category entry points and the 95-5 rule, as the LinkedIn B2B Institute describes them: most buyers are not in the market at any given time, so what matters is being linked to the situations that start a need. That research is about brands and advertising. Here it is applied to one person, so the list comes from your own past work, not a market study, and the output is signs to watch for. The failure it prevents is the message sent at a moment when nothing has happened, to someone who has no reason to read it.

## Workflow
1. Ask at most three questions: which engagements to draw on, how each one started, and which situations you suspect matter but have not worked on.
2. Write each buying situation as a moment beginning with "when": "when a co-founder leaves", not "leadership" or a service you sell. If it reads like a topic, rewrite it until something has happened in it.
3. Mark each one "seen in my work" (name the engagement, without the client name) or "assumed". Assumed situations stay on the map, visibly labelled.
4. For each situation, name the outside sign: what a stranger could see when it happens, and the kind of public page where it would show (a news page, a job advert, a leadership page, a talk). If nothing would show from outside, write "no outside sign": that situation can only reach you through someone who knows them.
5. Say for each sign how long it stays fresh, in your judgement. You set the window; I do not have a number for it.
6. Move every situation you have never worked on and cannot speak to honestly into "leave alone". Leaving it is a choice, not a gap to fill.
7. Add one plain line on time: most of the people you could help are not looking today, so the map is read over months and is not a forecast.

## Output Format
```markdown
# Buying Situations Map
[Your name], [date], built from [Ideal Client Profile or engagement notes]
## Situations
| When... (the moment) | Seen in my work or assumed | Outside sign you could spot | Where it would show | Fresh for (you set) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| When [event] | [seen: engagement, no name / assumed] | [what a stranger could see, or "no outside sign"] | [kind of public page] | [your window] |
## Leave alone
| When... (the moment) | Why you leave it |
|---|---|
| When [event] | [no work behind it / not for you] |
## How to read this map
Most people you could help are not looking today. Read this over months, not weeks.
## Decision
[Your name] chooses the two or three situations to look for first and the window for each, by [date].
```

## Done When
- Every situation starts with "when" and describes a moment, not a topic.
- Each one is marked seen or assumed, and every "seen" names an engagement without the client name.
- Each situation has an outside sign or says "no outside sign".
- No forecast, rate or percentage appears beyond the principle itself.

## Quality Bar
- Situations come from your real engagements, never from a list of popular topics.
- An outside sign is something public: nothing that needs a login, a bought data feed or a guess about what happens inside.
- The map names situations and roles, never named people.
- "Assumed" is an honest label, and it stays until real work replaces it.
- The map tells you what to look for; you choose who to reach and when.
