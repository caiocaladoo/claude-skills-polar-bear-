# Evidence and sources: Claude Guide for Outreach

Research checked 11 October 2026.

## What this guide claims

It claims that each skill follows a method or a working habit that is described in public, and that the rules about contacting people are taken from official pages and written as questions. It does not claim that any skill raises reply rates, books meetings or wins work. No such figure appears anywhere in the guide, because none could be traced to a primary source. Where a review skill counts results, the counts are yours.

## Deliberate design choices

These are Polar Bear's own choices, not findings from the sources.

- **The red line.** Claude researches and prepares; you choose the people, write every word and press send. The sources do not say who should write a message. This guide takes the position that the sender does.
- **A source for every fact.** A claim that cannot be tied to a public page or to your own first-hand history is dropped. Unknowns are listed, and a thin result is labelled thin. This is stricter than any source requires.
- **No scoring of people.** Organisations are checked against your brief with yes, no or unknown. Nobody is scored, ranked, tiered or typed, and the log holds no ratings.
- **Read only.** The browser and the mail and calendar connectors can act. This guide uses them to read, never on LinkedIn, and never to draft, send, book or post.
- **Small runs, warm first.** About ten people at a time, and a warm path check before any cold message. The size is a working habit, not a tested number.
- **Adviser referrals.** Every point about consent, anti-spam rules or data protection is a question drawn from an official page. Check with a qualified adviser before you rely on any of it.
- **Roles only.** The guide names the reader's role and nothing about the size, sector or type of anyone's organisation.
