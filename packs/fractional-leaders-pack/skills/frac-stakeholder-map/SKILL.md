# Stakeholder Map

## When To Use
You know the CEO well and nobody else, and the work depends on four other people. The finance lead holds the numbers, a team lead carries the function on your off days, a board member asks the questions, and you have met each once. This answers: who does the outcome depend on, what has each said they need, and where do I have no line at all?

## When Not To Use
If the person who hired you is leaving, run Sponsor Change Plan. If one working relationship has already gone cold over a missed commitment, run Relationship Repair Plan. This map does not rate, rank or profile anyone.

## Inputs
- The people you deal with or will need, by name and role, and when you last spoke to each
- What each has said they need, with where they said it (meeting, message, document)
- The outcome and the plan, so dependencies can be tied to dated work
If you have none of this, I start from the org chart or your memory of the first meetings, mark every stated need "unknown" and mark the map as a first draft.

## Approach
A two-way dependency map by role and stated need, a practitioner convention. Each person is placed by their role in the work, never on a grid of power or attitude; grids of that kind are left out on purpose, because they turn impressions into labels. The judgment is to write only what someone said and to turn every blank into a question you ask in person. The failure this prevents is the single thread: the whole engagement hangs on the CEO's diary and nobody else can say why your seat matters. Works well in Projects (beta), one Project per client, or a plain chat.

## Workflow
1. Ask at most three questions: who does the outcome depend on besides the CEO; whom have you not yet met; where has work already waited on someone?
2. Place each person by role in the work: decides, funds, uses the result, can block, advises. One person may hold two roles; write both.
3. Record what each has said they need, with the source and date. A need you inferred is written "inferred" and becomes a question. Nothing is recorded about personality, mood or loyalty.
4. Write the dependency both ways, with dates: what you need from them (data, a decision, cover on your off days) and what they need from you. Late dependencies on either side are where the work stalls.
5. Mark the gaps: not met, reached only through the CEO, no stated need, no contact since [date]. Each gap becomes a question or an introduction to ask for.
6. Choose the first three conversations and what you will ask in each. Update the map after every conversation; it is yours, and it is not shared.

## Output Format
```markdown
# Stakeholder Map
Client: [name] · Outcome: [one line] · Updated: [date]
## People by role in the work
| Person | Role in the work | What they said they need (source, date) | I need from them, by when | They need from me, by when | Last contact | Gap |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| [name, title] | [decides / funds / uses / can block / advises] | [need, source] or unknown | [item, date] | [item, date] | [date] | [none / not met / only through the CEO / no stated need] |
## Unknowns turned into questions
| Unknown | Question | Ask whom | By when |
|---|---|---|---|
| [what you do not know] | [open question] | [person] | [date] |
## First conversations
1. [person]: [what you will ask] · [date]
## Decision
[You] decide which three gaps to close first, and ask [CEO] for the introductions you need by [date].
```

## Done When
- Every person appears by role and stated need, with no rating of any kind
- Every stated need has a source, or reads "unknown" or "inferred"
- Dependencies run both ways and carry dates
- Each gap has a question and a person to ask

## Quality Bar
- No power, influence, attitude or sentiment label on anyone, in any column
- Unknown stays unknown; I never guess what someone wants or thinks
- Nothing private, and nothing a person told you in confidence, goes on the map
- The map is your working page; it is not sent to the client
- Only what you give me is recorded, nothing is invented, and the map holds one client's people and never another's
