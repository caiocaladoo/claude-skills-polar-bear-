# Weekly CEO Update

## When To Use
Your update is a list of meetings and the CEO still asks what you did. On two days a week, the CEO sees little of the work, so the update is most of what they know about the seat. This answers: what moved against the plan, what is off, and what do I need from you by when?

## When Not To Use
If something has just turned and cannot wait for the weekly rhythm, run Early Warning Note today. If you need the month against what was agreed, with the scoreboard, run Monthly Value Report.

## Inputs
- This client's log for the week: decisions, commitments, days used, scope asks
- The 90-day plan: objectives, measures, targets and the current figures with their source
- With the Gmail connector I can place the draft in your drafts folder; I never send it
If you have none of this, I start from what you tell me moved this week, mark every figure "to confirm" and mark the update as a first draft.

## Approach
Bottom line first, then status against the plan, a plain-writing convention described generically: the reader gets the conclusion in the first line and the support underneath. The judgment is to report results, and to report red the week it is true. The failure this prevents is the activity list ("met with sales, reviewed the pipeline, joined the offsite"), which reads as busy and tells the CEO nothing about whether the outcome is nearer.

## Workflow
1. Ask at most three questions: what is the one thing the CEO should know this week; what do you need them to decide; did any ask this week go beyond the agreed days?
2. Write the bottom line in one or two sentences: the main result, or the main problem. If the week went badly, the first line says so.
3. For each objective in the plan, give the status as it is this week, the figure with its source, and what moved it. Where nothing moved, write that. Meetings appear only when a decision came out of them.
4. List the decisions you need from the CEO, each with the options in a line and the date after which the work waits.
5. Name the scope asks that arrived this week and go beyond the agreed days, with the baseline line each touches. They are stated here, not absorbed and not argued.
6. Close with next week: the one or two results you are working toward and what you need. Keep it to a screen. You edit it and send it yourself.

## Output Format
```markdown
# Weekly CEO Update
Client: [name] · Week of: [date] · From: [you] · Days used this month: [n] of [n]
## Bottom line
[One or two sentences: the main result or the main problem]
## Against the plan
| Objective | Status this week | Figure (source) | What moved it |
|---|---|---|---|
| [objective] | [on track / at risk / off track] | [figure, system] | [effect, or "nothing moved"] |
## Decisions I need from you
| Decision | Options in a line | By when |
|---|---|---|
| [decision] | [options] | [date] |
## Asks beyond the agreed days
- [ask, from whom, date] · touches: [statement of work or charter line] · proposed: [scope change request / swap / defer]
## Coming week
- [result you are working toward] · I need: [what, from whom]
## Decision
[CEO] answers the listed decisions by [date]; [you] send this update and raise anything urgent in person.
```

## Done When
- The first line is a result or a problem, never a list of activity
- Every figure has a source in your log or the client's systems
- Each decision asked has a date
- The update fits on one screen

## Quality Bar
- Status is reported as it is this week; nothing stays "on track" to avoid a conversation
- No adjective does the work of a figure
- Team members are named for decisions and ownership, never assessed
- Scope asks are stated neutrally, with no comment on the asker's motive
- Facts come only from this client's log, nothing is invented or borrowed from another client, and you send the update, not Claude
