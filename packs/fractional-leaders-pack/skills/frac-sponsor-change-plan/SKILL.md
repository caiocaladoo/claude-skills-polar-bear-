# Sponsor Change Plan

## When To Use
The person who hired you is leaving, or you realise only one person there knows why you matter. A part-time seat has few witnesses. When the sponsor goes, their successor inherits a line in the budget and a name they do not know. This answers: how do I meet the new sponsor on their goals, and who else should be able to say what the seat does?

## When Not To Use
If no one is leaving and you want the whole picture of who the work depends on, run Stakeholder Map. If the contract date is what worries you, run Renewal Review.

## Inputs
- Who is leaving, when, and who takes over, if known
- What the new sponsor has said about their priorities, with where and when they said it
- Your monthly reports, decision records and stakeholder map for this client
If you have none of this, I start from the date of the change and what the engagement has delivered so far, mark the new sponsor's goals "unknown" and mark the plan as a first draft.

## Approach
Five steps for a change of sponsor from customer success practice (ask for an introduction, hold a knowledge transfer, make contact, learn the new person's goals, revise the plan), joined to a roles table from key account planning, described generically: people by their role in the work and what they have said they need. The source is old and was written for account managers, so it is used as a checklist, not as evidence about fractional work. The failure this prevents is the single thread.

## Workflow
1. Ask at most three questions: who is leaving and on what date; do you know the successor; who besides the sponsor has seen the results of your work?
2. Ask for the introduction while the sponsor is still there. I draft the request for you to send: a short note, with the two things the successor should hear from the person who hired you.
3. Prepare the three-way handover call: why the seat exists, what was agreed (statement of work, charter), what has been delivered against it, what is open, what the sponsor would do next.
4. Write what the new sponsor has said about their goals, with the source. Anything they have not said is "unknown" and becomes a question for your first meeting. I do not guess their view of you, of the seat or of part-time roles.
5. Run the single-thread check: for each result, who else at the client can describe it? List the relationships to build, by role, and what each person needs from your function.
6. Revise the next 90 days against the new sponsor's stated goals, marking what continues, what waits for their decision and what you propose to stop.

## Output Format
```markdown
# Sponsor Change Plan
Client: [name] · Leaving: [role, name] on [date] · Taking over: [role, name, or not yet known]
## Introduction and handover call
Introduction asked of [sponsor] by [date] · Call agenda: why the seat exists · what was agreed · delivered against it · open items · what [sponsor] would do next
## The new sponsor's goals
| Goal or priority | In their words? (source, date) | If unknown: question to ask |
|---|---|---|
| [goal] | [stated, where] or unknown | [open question] |
## Who else knows what the seat does
| Result or piece of work | Who can describe it today (role) | Relationship to build | What they need from the function |
|---|---|---|---|
| [result] | [role, or only the sponsor] | [role] | [stated need, or unknown] |
## Revised next 90 days
| Work | Continues / waits for their decision / propose to stop |
|---|---|
| [work] | [status] |
## Decision
[You] ask [sponsor] for the introduction by [date]; [new sponsor] confirms or changes the priorities at the first meeting.
```

## Done When
- The introduction is asked for before the sponsor's last day
- Every goal of the new sponsor is sourced or reads "unknown" with a question
- Each result has at least one other person who can describe it, or a relationship to build
- The revised plan marks what waits for the new sponsor's decision

## Quality Bar
- People appear by role and stated need; nobody is rated as ally, risk or obstacle
- No guess about the new sponsor's opinion or intentions
- The handover call reports what was delivered from your reports, with nothing rounded up
- The plan is not a campaign to keep the contract; it tells the new sponsor what they need to decide
- Only this client's records are used, nothing is invented, and you make the introduction request and hold the meetings yourself
