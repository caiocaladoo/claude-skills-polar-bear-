# Retainer Conversation Prep

## When To Use
The fee call is tomorrow and you know you will offer money off before anyone asks. The CEO pauses, and you fill the silence with a cheaper month. This page answers one question before you dial: what is my number, what do I walk away at, and what will I trade instead of the fee?

## When Not To Use
If you are raising the fee of a client you already serve, run Retainer Increase Plan. If the CEO has just said AI covers half the seat, run AI Objection Answer. If you have no number yet, run Good-Better-Best Engagement Options and Fractional Pricing Models first.

## Inputs
- The option and the fee you will put forward, and your own floor (with Memory on, I can keep your floor between chats; it is yours, not client material)
- Your capacity for the period: what happens to those days if this does not close
- What the CEO has said about budget, timing and what they must show the board, in their words
If you have none of this, I start from your number and your floor alone and mark the output as a first draft.

## Approach
BATNA and interests, not positions, as the Harvard Program on Negotiation explains them on its public pages. Your best alternative to this engagement is what lets you stay calm at the pause, so it is written down honestly, compared on equal terms, and it changes how much you concede, not what you say. The failure this prevents is the pre-emptive discount: the retainer falls in the first minute because you feared a "no" nobody said.

## Workflow
1. Ask, all at once: what number are you putting forward, and for which option; what happens to those days next quarter if this does not close; what has the CEO said about budget, in their words?
2. Write your number and your walk-away floor side by side. The floor is internal; it never appears in anything the client sees.
3. Write your real alternative if there is no deal: the days sold to another client, a different engagement in view, or days left free. Compare it with this deal on money, time and effort. If it is weak, say so plainly.
4. List the CEO's interests behind their likely position (spend, risk, timing, proving the seat to the board), only from what they said. Add the questions that would teach you the rest. I never guess their budget.
5. Name the criteria that carry the fee: the outcome they described, the decision rights, the assumptions. Draft your first two sentences in your voice: the number, then what it buys. Then the questions you expect, each with an answer you can say out loud.
6. List what you can trade and what each costs you: fewer days with a smaller outcome, a phased start, payment terms, a first review date. Then the line you will not cross: the same seat for less.

## Output Format
```markdown
# Retainer Conversation Prep
Client: [name or code] · Call: [date] · Option: [which]
## Numbers (internal)
| Item | Figure | Source |
|---|---|---|
| My number | [your figure] | [option, model] |
| Walk-away floor | [your figure] | [your own floor] |
| My alternative if no deal | [what, in money and days] | [capacity plan] |
## Their interests
| What I know (their words) | Interest behind it | Question to learn more |
|---|---|---|
| [as said] | [spend / risk / timing / proof for the board] | [open question] |
## What I say
- Criteria that carry the fee: [outcome, decision rights, assumption]
- First two sentences: [the number] [what it buys] · Expected question: [question] · My answer: [answer]
## Trades
| I can offer | It costs me | I ask in return |
|---|---|---|
| [days, phasing, terms] | [cost] | [what you value] |
Not on the table: the same seat for less.
## Decision
[You] decide before [call date] the lowest point you accept and which trades you offer; you have the conversation yourself.
```

## Done When
- Number, floor and alternative come from your own records, or are marked missing
- Every interest traces to something the CEO said
- The first two sentences state the number before any explanation, and each trade has a cost to you and something asked in return

## Quality Bar
- The floor never leaves the internal section
- No tactic scripts: no anchoring tricks, no staged silence, no deadline pressure
- The CEO is described by interests, never by personality
- No market rate is offered as a reason, and no other client's fee or terms are mentioned or used
- Claude prepares the page from your numbers; it invents none, and you have the conversation yourself
