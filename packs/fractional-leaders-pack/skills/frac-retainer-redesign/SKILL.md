# Retainer Redesign

## When To Use
The role has outgrown, or shrunk below, the days you sold. Either the change log is full and you are doing a larger job on the old terms, or the work that took two days now takes one because of AI and you both know it. Use this to decide what the retainer should buy from now on, and how you will explain the change.

## When Not To Use
If the shape is right and only the level is wrong, run Retainer Increase Plan. If you are shaping a brand new engagement, run Good-Better-Best Engagement Options. If the CEO has only raised a doubt, run AI Objection Answer first.

## Inputs
- The statement of work for this client: days bought, fee, notice terms
- Days used and what was delivered over a period you choose, from the log and monthly reports, and the change log
- What the client says they value, in their words, and where your own use of AI changed the time the work takes
If you have none of this, I start from the days bought and last quarter's delivery list and mark the output as a first draft.

## Approach
Retainers sit on a line from input (the client buys your days) through output (a set of deliverables) to outcome (a result), a common way of sorting fee models, described generically. The judgment is to match the type to how measurable the result is and how stable the scope is for this client, not to what sounds modern. The failure this prevents is a retainer that reports fewer days every month until the CEO asks for a cut you never planned for, or one that quietly doubles while the fee stays still.

## Workflow
1. Ask at most three questions: which retainer, and over what period; has the CEO already raised the days or the fee; what do you want to be doing for this client in six months?
2. Lay out the retainer as it stands: days bought, days used, what was delivered, asks absorbed. Mark where AI cut your time and where it added review time. Your own days only; no team member's hours appear.
3. Write what the client values in their words. If you do not know, write "to ask"; a redesign built on a guessed value is a days retainer under a new name.
4. Set out five options and what the client gets in each: an outcome retainer (fee tied to a result both sides can see), capacity with an output floor (named outputs each month), a fixed-term handover (you build their capability and leave), advisory (a few hours of judgment, no ownership), or keep as is. Keep as is is a real answer where they value having you in the room.
5. You choose the move and write why in one line. Any change to notice or contract terms goes on the adviser list.
6. Draft the conversation outline: what changes, why (including, plainly, how AI changed the work), what the client gets, from when. You hold the conversation.

## Done When
- Days, deliveries and absorbed asks come from your records, and the client's value is in their words or marked "to ask"
- All five options say what the client gets, not only what it costs
- The outline names how AI changed the work

## Quality Bar
- Options differ in what the client gets, never only in the fee
- No fee is proposed by Claude; you write each number, and no team member's hours or output are measured anywhere
- The AI line is true in both directions: time saved and review time added
- Only this client's records are used, nothing is invented or borrowed from another client's terms, and you explain the change yourself
