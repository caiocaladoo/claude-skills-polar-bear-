# Renewal Review

## When To Use
The contract is about to roll over, or run out, and nobody has looked at results. Either it renews by habit, with every drift of the last months carried forward, or it ends in a short message nobody saw coming. This answers, for both sides: what did the seat deliver, what did it cost in days, and what shape should it have next, if any?

## When Not To Use
If a decision to change the shape is already taken, run Retainer Redesign. If the only open point is one extra ask, run Scope Change Request. If you have no monthly pages to draw on, start with Monthly Value Report.

## Inputs
- The statement of work, the charter and the 90-day plan for this client, and your monthly reports
- Days used against days agreed across the period, and your own count of strategic against reactive time
- What the CEO and others have said they value, in their words, with where they said it
If you have none of this, I start from what was agreed and what you can show was delivered, mark what the client values "to ask" and mark the review as a first draft.

## Approach
A stay, scale or sunset review, as a public playbook for fractional executives calls the decision at day 90 and at each quarter; an agency's article adds that the review date belongs in the contract from the start, and a fractional marketplace's guidance adds the leader's own audit (hours, strategic against reactive time, and whether you would sign again). The judgment is that the review has two deciders: the CEO decides whether to renew, and you decide whether to offer. The failure this prevents is a renewal that turns into a debate about hours because nobody put results on the table.

## Workflow
1. Ask at most three questions: when does the contract roll over or end; what has the CEO said about the seat lately, in their words; if you were offered this engagement today on the same terms, would you take it?
2. Set results against the outcome and each objective, with evidence and the same "shown" or "contributed, not attributable" marks as in the monthly reports. Missed objectives are listed first.
3. Set days used against days agreed for the period, and your split of strategic against reactive time. You judge what the split means; I apply no threshold.
4. Write what the client values in their words, with the source. Where you do not know, write "to ask" and add the question to the review meeting.
5. Run your own check, privately: would I sign this again at this scope and fee, and what would have to change? This section stays with you.
6. Lay out five options, each with what it would mean in outcome, days and decision rights: continue as is, grow the seat, shrink to advisory, convert to a full-time hire (with a handover), or end. Say honestly where a full-time hire, or the team with its own AI, now fits better than you.

## Output Format
```markdown
# Renewal Review
Client: [name] · Period reviewed: [dates] · Contract date: [date] · Review meeting: [date]
## Results against the charter
| Outcome or objective | Result | Evidence | Shown / contributed, not attributable |
|---|---|---|---|
| [objective] | [result, or missed] | [report, figure, decision] | [mark] |
## Days and kind of work
Days agreed: [n] · Days used: [n] · Strategic: [your count] · Reactive: [your count] · Scope asks absorbed: [n, from the log]
## What the client values
| In their words | Who (role), where | Or: to ask |
|---|---|---|
| [quote as given] | [role, meeting] | [question] |
## Options
| Option | Outcome owned | Days | Decision rights | Fits because |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Continue / grow / shrink to advisory / convert to full-time hire / end | [..] | [..] | [..] | [reason from the evidence] |
## My own check (not shared)
Would I sign this again as it stands? [yes / no / with changes: which]
## Decision
[CEO] decides whether to renew and in which shape by [date]; [you] decide which shapes you offer before the meeting on [date].
```

## Done When
- Every objective has a result and evidence, missed ones included
- Days used are set against days agreed for the whole period
- What the client values is quoted with a source, or reads "to ask"
- All five options are described, including ending and the full-time hire

## Quality Bar
- Results are the ones in your monthly reports; nothing is improved for the meeting
- Where another option now serves the company better than your seat, the page says so
- The review describes the engagement, never the worth of the people in it
- Notice periods and renewal terms: check with a qualified adviser
- No result, figure or quote is invented or taken from another client, and you hold the review conversation yourself
