# Handover Note

## When To Use
The engagement ends in a month and everything is still in your head. The team can run the routine but cannot explain why it is built that way, and "quick questions" will follow you for a quarter after the end date. Start it 90 days out if you can, 30 if you must. It answers: what does the next owner need in order to carry on without calling you?

## When Not To Use
If the question is still whether the engagement continues, run Renewal Review. If you only need one decision written up, run Decision Record.

## Inputs
- This client's log, decision records, monthly reports and the 90-day plan
- Who takes over each area, by name and role, or "not yet known"
- The end date, your access list, and what the contract says about materials and later advice
If you have none of this, I start from the end date and a list of what you run today, and mark the note as a first draft.

## Approach
Knowledge transfer on a timeline that starts well before the last day, as a fractional marketplace's guidance and a public playbook for fractional executives both describe it: document what exists, hand over decisions with their reasons, move relationships by introduction, then remove your own access. The judgment is to write for the person who was not there. The failure this prevents is the exit nobody planned: the strategy leaves with you, and the team keeps the routines without the reasons.

## Workflow
1. Ask at most three questions: what is the end date; who takes over which area; what would break first if you were unreachable the week after?
2. List what was built and where it lives: plans, processes, templates, reports, each with its location and its owner from now on.
3. Attach the decision log: each decision that still shapes the work, with its reason and its review date. Then open work, each line with an owner, the next step and its date.
4. Hand over relationships by name and role, inside and outside the company, each with an introduction you will make and by when. Write what the next owner should not change yet, and why, with the date or condition after which they can.
5. List your access and materials: what is removed and when, what is returned or deleted as the contract says. Nothing from this client goes with you into another client's work.
6. Count the timeline back from the end date (no new work accepted, transfer sessions, documentation complete, final sign-offs, access removed) and state the terms of any later advice. If a full-time hire follows, I write a brief of the role: its outcome, decisions and first 90 days. I do not assess or compare candidates.

## Output Format
```markdown
# Handover Note
Client: [name] · End date: [date] · Handing over to: [name, role, or not yet known]
## What was built and where it lives
| Item | Location | Owner from now on |
|---|---|---|
| [plan, process, template, report] | [where] | [name, role] |
## Decisions that still shape the work
| Decision | Reason | Record | Review date |
|---|---|---|---|
| [decision] | [why] | [link to the decision record] | [date] |
## Open work and relationships
| Open work or relationship | Owner or contact (name, role) | Next step or introduction | By |
|---|---|---|---|
| [item] | [name, role] | [step] | [date] |
Do not change yet: [what] until [date or condition], because [reason]
## Access, materials and timeline
| Counting back from the end date | Date |
|---|---|
| No new work accepted · transfer sessions · documentation complete · sign-offs · access removed and materials returned or deleted | [dates] |
Later advice: [terms agreed, or none] · If a hire follows, role brief: [outcome, decisions, first 90 days]
## Decision
[CEO] names the owner of each area by [date] and signs off the handover on [date]; [you] remove your access on [date].
```

## Done When
- Every item built has a location and an owner from now on
- Every open line has an owner, a next step and a date
- Each relationship has an introduction with a date, and your access removal and the return or deletion of materials are dated

## Quality Bar
- Written for someone who was not there: reasons, not only instructions
- People appear by name and role with what they own, never with an assessment; a successor is described as a role, and no candidate is evaluated
- What happens to client materials and any later advice follows the contract: check with a qualified adviser
- Nothing is invented to fill a gap, and no document, figure or template from this client is carried into another client's work
