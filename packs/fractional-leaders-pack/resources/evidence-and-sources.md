# Evidence and sources

Research checked 11 October 2026.

## What this pack claims

The pack claims that each skill follows a named public method, or a plainly described practitioner convention, and that the situations it answers are ones fractional and interim executives and their clients describe in public writing. It contains no day rates, no retainer levels, no benchmarks and no client results: every number in a finished output comes from the person using it or from the client's own records. The sources below give their own rules of thumb (hours per week, percentages, fee ranges); none of them is used in any skill.

## Deliberate design choices

These are Polar Bear's own choices, not findings from the sources. The red line (nothing invented, no client's information moved into another client's work, the leader speaks for themselves) is ours; the sources say little about keeping several clients' material apart, and we treat it as the risk that defines this role. No skill grades, ranks or scores a person: the stakeholder map and the sponsor plan record roles and what people have said, power and influence grids are left out, and a successor is described as a role, never as a candidate. No rate, retainer level, market benchmark or capacity percentage from any source is repeated; where a source gives a number as a rule of thumb, the skill asks for the leader's own records instead. "Do it in house with AI" appears as a real option because clients raise it, and the leader's own AI use is written into the statement of work and the charter. Contract, contractor status, tax, equity and notice points are listed as questions for a qualified adviser and never answered. The pack names the role only, with no company size, sector or type.
