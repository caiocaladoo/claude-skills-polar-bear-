# Evidence and sources

Research checked 11 October 2026.

## What this pack claims

**It claims** that a product manager, a product designer or a founder can take one idea to a prototype real users can try, by following a fixed order of small artifacts: a question, a pass line, a spec, a prototype, a set of checks before sharing, five real sessions, a handover and a decision. Each skill applies a method that is public and attributed below.

The Claude surfaces named in the skills (Claude Design, Claude Docs, artifacts, Claude Code, connectors, custom skills) are described only as far as Anthropic's own pages describe them on the date above. Product pages change. Open the page before you rely on a detail.

## Deliberate design choices

These are 90n's choices, not findings from the sources.

- **The red line.** "Claude builds the prototype, real users test it and engineers own what ships: a prototype is never called production, and nothing holding real customer data or a live key is shared until a named person has checked it." The GOV.UK guidance points the same way; the pack is stricter. "Ship the prototype" is never an option on any decision line.
- **The order.** Eight stages in a fixed order, with the pass line written before the prototype exists, is our arrangement. No source prescribes it.
- **The safe-to-show group.** Three checks (keys, personal data, who can open the link) and a one-page sign-off by a named person are our arrangement of points from OWASP, the UK ICO, GOV.UK and Anthropic's sharing pages. Turning engineering references into questions a builder asks ("show me where the key lives") is our choice.
- **No people scoring.** Severity rates usability problems. Participants appear as codes, results as counts across a round, and nothing grades, ranks or profiles a tester, a colleague or an engineer.
- **Adviser referrals.** Security points end with "check with your engineering or security lead". Legal, privacy, consent and recording points end with "check with a qualified adviser". The pack states principles and questions, never advice.
- **Roles only.** The pack names the reader's role and nothing else about where they work, in the skills and in every example.
- **Real users are not optional.** Colleagues, the leaders who asked for the idea and simulated participants never count as testers. This is a position, supported by the testing guidance but stated more firmly than any source states it.
