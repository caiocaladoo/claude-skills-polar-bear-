# Evidence and sources

Research checked 11 October 2026.

## What this guide claims

**It claims:** each skill applies a named method or produces a standard artifact of evaluation and reporting work; each named method is attributed to its originator through a public page, an institution guide or a peer-reviewed paper that opened on the date above (or on the earlier date marked beside it); and the sources below support the design choices the skills make: agree the measures before the build, capture the before figures, collect evidence at more than one level, separate what the programme did from what else changed, and report for the reader's decision.

## Deliberate design choices

These are 90n's choices, not findings from the sources. The red line is ours: Claude plans the evaluation and drafts the report from your data: it never invents a result, a benchmark or a quote, never claims a cause the evidence does not support, never turns evidence about a programme into a judgement of a person, and a named person stands behind every number. So are the rules that follow from it. The measures agreement is signed before the build, as a gate. Every claim carries one of three strengths (shown, suggested, unknown), a scale of our own. The overclaim check is the last step before any report leaves. Evidence is collected and reported for groups, with a minimum group size the user sets, and no instrument records a name. The observation checklist has three marks per behaviour (seen, not seen, no occasion) and no rating scale; a person fills it, and it describes the programme, never the person observed or their manager. The Success Case Method is reframed: where the original looks for the most and least successful participants, this guide picks uses of the training from follow-up survey answers, among volunteers, with consent, and writes them up anonymously, so a case is never chosen by who the person is. The CIPD factsheet includes an individual level of needs analysis and performance data as a source; this guide stays at role and task level and takes no appraisal data. The post-training survey rates the programme and never the trainer. The return on investment is a range from the user's own figures, and the skill stops when there is no evidence of use on the job. A note for the programme that did not work is part of the guide. Privacy, consent and data protection points are marked "check with a qualified adviser". The guide names the role it is for and nothing else about the reader.
