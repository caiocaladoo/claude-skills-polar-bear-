# Behaviour Observation Checklist

## When To Use
Surveys say people use it and you want to know whether it shows up in the work. Use this to give a person who will watch the work a sheet that records one thing only: on this occasion, was the programme's behaviour seen, not seen, or was there no occasion for it?

## When Not To Use
This is not a way to assess anyone. If someone wants to know how a particular person, team or manager is doing, this sheet cannot tell them and I will not adapt it to. If the behaviours leave no visible trace, or people cannot be told they are being observed, use Training Follow-Up Survey and Learner Interview Guide instead.

## Inputs
- The critical behaviours and their "seen at work" column from your Critical Behaviours Map
- The groups to be observed, the settings where the occasions come up, the role of each observer, and the minimum reporting group you set
If you have none of this, I start from the two or three things people should visibly do differently, as you describe them, and mark the output "no agreement on file: first draft".

## Approach
This is structured observation with a checklist, applied to the Level 3 critical behaviours of the Kirkpatrick Model as set out by Kirkpatrick Partners. The "what blocked it" notes are read with the transfer research: in a meta-analysis by Blume, Ford, Baldwin and Huang (2010), use on the job relates to a supportive work environment, a correlational finding. The sheet is deliberately poor. Three marks, no name, a count per group. The moment an observation form gains a rating scale or a name field, it stops being evidence about a programme and becomes a file about a person, and everyone observed knows it.

## Workflow
1. Ask three questions at once: which critical behaviours from the map will be observed, which groups and settings and by which observer roles, and what minimum reporting group do you set?
2. Write one row per behaviour as something visible, in the form "checks [X] before [Y]". I add no behaviour that is not on the map, and anything that needs a judgement of quality to mark is rewritten or sent back to the map.
3. Fix the marks: seen, not seen, no occasion. There is no scale, no score, no "partly" and no quality rating. "No occasion" matters most: it separates "did not do it" from "never had the chance", which is a fact about the work.
4. Set the header: group, date, setting, observer's role. There is no field for a name, initials, an employee number or any detail that singles a person out, and I refuse to add one.
5. Add one "what blocked it" line per behaviour, for conditions only: a tool, time, a process, a missing job aid. Never a person's attitude or ability.
6. Build the tally template: across all sheets for a group at or above the minimum, the count of each mark per behaviour. The share is "seen" out of "seen plus not seen"; "no occasion" is reported beside it and never folded in. A person fills every sheet and every count. I never fill a mark, never infer one from other data and never observe anyone.
7. Write the briefing lines and the limits. People are told beforehand that they are being observed and why. The tally states that people act differently when watched and that a handful of occasions is not a rate.

## Output Format
```markdown
# Behaviour Observation Checklist
Programme: [name] | This sheet describes the programme. It is not about you, and no name is written on it.
## Observation sheet (blank, filled by the observer)
Group: [group] | Date: [date] | Setting: [setting] | Observer's role: [role]
| # | Behaviour as something visible | Seen | Not seen | No occasion | What blocked it (conditions only) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | [checks [X] before [Y]] | [ ] | [ ] | [ ] | [tool, time, process, missing job aid] |
## What people are told beforehand
[Why the work is being observed, that no name is recorded, that sheets are tallied for the group, who to ask.]
## Group tally (filled by a person, groups at or above [minimum you set])
| Behaviour | Group | Sheets | Seen | Not seen | No occasion | Seen out of seen plus not seen | Voucher |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| [behaviour] | [group] | [count] | [count] | [count] | [count] | [your figure, or "too few occasions"] | [name, or "no voucher yet"] |
## Blocking conditions across sheets
| Condition | Behaviours affected | Who (role) could remove it |
|---|---|---|
| [condition] | [behaviour numbers] | [role] |

Limits: people act differently when watched. [Count] occasions is not a rate. Sheets go to [L&D role] only.
## Decision
[L&D lead name] decides by [date] whether observation goes ahead in this form, and [named person] vouches for the tally by [date].
```

## Done When
- Every row is a behaviour from the map that an observer could mark without judging quality
- The sheet carries exactly three marks and a header of group, date, setting and observer's role; the tally reports "no occasion" beside the share, for groups at or above your minimum only
- The briefing lines and both limits are written on the output, and every mark and count is left blank

## Quality Bar
- Blocking notes name conditions; a note about a person is struck out and rewritten as the condition behind it
- Sheets never go to a personnel file, an appraisal or a manager's review, and the output says so
- Requests to add names, compare individuals, or rank teams or their managers are refused in one line, with the group tally offered instead
- Consent, privacy and employee representation points: check with a qualified adviser
- The checklist describes the programme, never the person observed or their manager: three marks, no name, a tally by group, filled by a person
