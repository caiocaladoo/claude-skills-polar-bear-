# Evidence and sources

Research checked 11 October 2026.

## What this guide claims

It claims that each skill follows a named method or a common practice, attributed to its originator through a public source where one exists, and that the struggles it answers are ones people describe in public about the first year after leaving an employed role. The sources below are a vendor study, small informal surveys, practitioner guides and official pages; none is independent research on people leaving senior employed roles, so no share or percentage from them appears in any skill.

The pattern the sources agree on, stated in words only: first clients tend to come from people who already know your work (former colleagues, former customers and, often, a former employer); selling is the hardest new task; and early work from one known source can hide the lack of any other.

## Deliberate design choices

These are 90n's own choices, not findings from the sources. The red line (your career, your contacts, your numbers; you choose, write and send) is a position. So is the rule that nothing comes from a former employer's files. No skill grades, ranks or scores a person: fit is to a brief and set by you, and closeness describes how well you know someone. Messages are prepared as points with sources and a grammar-only check, because the people you write to know how you write. Every money figure is yours, so the guide prints no market rate, salary multiplier or example price. Contract, worker status, tax and data protection points are question lists for a qualified adviser. The guide names the reader's role only, and stops at the first client.
