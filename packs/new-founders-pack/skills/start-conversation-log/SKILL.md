# Conversation Log

## When To Use
You told three people "I will send you that" and cannot remember which three. In a job, a system and a team caught those; now a dropped promise is the whole impression someone has of your new practice. This log answers: what is still open, and who am I due to get back to?

## When Not To Use
If you want to decide what to do this week, the log feeds Weekly Selling Hour and is not a to-do list. If you want notes on how keen or how senior someone seemed, that is not kept here or anywhere in this guide.

## Inputs
- Your own notes from a call, a coffee chat or a reply, in any form
- The existing log, if you have one
- Where it lives: a private Project, or a spreadsheet through file creation that you keep yourself. Never a shared space.
If you have none of this, I start from what you remember of your last conversation and mark the output as a first draft.

## Approach
A contact log is old, plain practice. Its risk is equally old: notes about people slowly turn into profiles of them. So this log holds two kinds of thing only, facts about a conversation and promises, yours and theirs. No sales system is needed for a first year of conversations; a table you actually keep is enough. The failure it prevents is the quiet one: you said "I will introduce you to someone who has done exactly this", meant it, and three weeks later they have stopped expecting it.

## Workflow
1. Ask at most three questions: who you spoke to and when, what kind of conversation it was, and whether anything was promised on either side.
2. Write one row per conversation: date, who, context, what they said they need (paraphrased in your words, never pasted from a message), what you promised and by when, what they promised, and the next date you set.
3. Pull every promise into the open list, yours first. A promise without a date gets one now, set by you; "soon" is how introductions get lost.
4. Strip anything that is not a fact or a promise: ratings, "warm" or "cold", impressions, guesses about budget or intent, anything personal shared in confidence. I flag those lines and leave them out.
5. Mark promises kept with the date you kept them, and keep the row; the monthly review counts what you did.
6. Check where the log lives. If any copy sits somewhere shared, including a former employer's tools, move it to your private place before adding more.

## Output Format
```markdown
# Conversation Log
Kept in: [my private Project or file] | Last updated: [date]
## Open promises
| Who | What was promised | By whom | By when | Status |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| [name] | [introduction, document, answer] | [me / them] | [date] | [open / kept on date] |
## Conversations
| Date | Who | Context | What they said they need | What I promised | What they promised | Next date |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| [date] | [name] | [call / coffee chat / reply] | [my paraphrase] | [promise and date, or none] | [promise, or none] | [date I set] |
## Left out
- [line removed: rating, impression or confidence]
## Decision
[Your name] decides by [date] which open promise to keep first, and sets each next date personally.
```

## Done When
- Every conversation row has a date, a context and a next date.
- Every promise has an owner and a date, and open promises are listed first.
- No rating, impression or confidential detail remains.
- The log sits in a private place only.

## Quality Bar
- Paraphrase in your words; never paste message or email text.
- What they said they need is about their work, never about them as a person.
- No column for warmth, value, likelihood or priority of a person.
- Holding notes on other people carries data protection duties: check with a qualified adviser.
- Facts and promises from your own conversations, kept private by you; no one is rated.
