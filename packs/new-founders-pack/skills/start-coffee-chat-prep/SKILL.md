# Coffee Chat Prep

## When To Use
Someone agreed to a call, and you are afraid of turning a catch-up into a sales meeting. You are new at this, they know it, and the temptation is to fill every silence with what you now offer. This answers: what will I ask, what can I give, and what will I deliberately not say?

## When Not To Use
If they have told you they have a problem and want to talk about help, this is no longer a coffee chat; prepare with Discovery Call Guide. If you have no call yet, start with Reconnect Message.

## Inputs
- Who they are, how you know them, and why they agreed to talk, in your words
- Public links about their work, and your last thread with them from your own account
- Your Positioning Statement line, and the time and length of the call as you type them
If you have none of this, I start from the person's name and why they agreed to talk, and mark the output as a first draft.

## Approach
This is informational interview practice, as university career offices teach it: a short conversation to learn about someone's work and world, not to ask for a job. Their rules carry over well. Say what you hope to learn, let the other person do most of the talking, do not ask for work, thank them and stay in touch. The plan is written before the call so the hard part is decided calmly: what you will not pitch. The failure it prevents: a good conversation, then a ten-minute answer to "so what are you doing now?" that turns the whole call into a sales meeting in hindsight.

## Workflow
1. Ask at most three questions: why they agreed to talk, how long the call is, and what you hope comes out of it, so it can be written down and then held loosely.
2. Pick three to five facts about their work, each with its source. Drop anything you would be uncomfortable saying you looked up, and anything personal.
3. Write three questions you want answered, open and about their work and their world: what has changed, what is hard, what they would do in your place. Questions research could not answer go first. Test each: would you still ask it with nothing to sell?
4. Choose one useful thing to offer: an introduction, something to read, an hour of your experience on a problem they named. One, not three.
5. Write what you will not pitch. If they ask what you do, you say your one line, in your own words, then turn back to them. If they ask how they can help, you have one specific answer ready: a role in a situation you would like to meet.
6. Plan the close: thank them, make the offer, and suggest a next step only if one came up naturally. "No next step" is a valid close.
7. Note the hand-off: the same day, you log what was said and promised, nothing else.

## Output Format
```markdown
# Coffee Chat Plan
With: [name] | When: [date, time, length] | Why they agreed: [my words]
## What I know
| Fact about their work | Source |
|---|---|
| [fact] | [link and date, or our thread of date] |
## Three questions I want answered
1. [open question research could not answer]
2. [open question about their work]
3. [open question about how they see my field from where they sit]
## One thing I can offer
- [what]
## What I will not pitch
- [my words]
- If asked what I do: [my line, said my way]
- If asked how they can help: [a role in a situation I would like to meet]
## How I would close
- Thank: [my words] | Offer: [the one thing] | Next step, only if it came up: [or "none"]
## Decision
[Your name] decides at the end of the call whether to suggest a next step, and logs what was said and promised by [date].
```

## Done When
- Every fact listed has its source, and you are comfortable saying you found it.
- The three questions are open and about their work.
- One offer and the "will not pitch" lines are written before the call.
- The close allows for no next step.

## Quality Bar
- Questions are curiosity, never a disguised sales script.
- The plan holds prompts, not lines to read aloud.
- Nothing private from the research reaches the conversation.
- No offer of your services unless they ask, and then one line.
- Claude prepares from public work facts and your own thread; you hold the conversation as yourself.
