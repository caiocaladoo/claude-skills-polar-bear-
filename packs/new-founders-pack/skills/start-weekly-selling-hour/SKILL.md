# Weekly Selling Hour

## When To Use
The first engagement started, and you have not written to anyone since. Delivery feels like the real work and it is paid; finding the next client is neither urgent nor comfortable. Use this once a week, same day, same length, to answer: what are the few things I do this week so that new conversations keep starting?

## When Not To Use
If you have no goal or weekly actions yet, run 90-Day Practice Plan first. To look at income, runway and how much one client holds, that is Monthly Practice Review; this hour acts on this week only.

## Inputs
- Last week's log line and your plan's weekly actions
- Your Conversation Log, and loose notes: calls, messages you meant to answer
- Things you sent and are waiting on, with dates: a recap, an offer page, a proposal, an introduction ask
- Optional: the Gmail connector on your own account, to search and read only
If you have none of this, I start from what you can recall in five minutes and mark the agenda as a first run.

## Approach
The agenda is the Weekly Review from the David Allen Company's checklist: get clear, get current, get creative. The full review covers a whole working life; here it is cut to one fixed slot for finding work only. The failure it prevents is the slot that turns into inbox cleaning and ends with nothing booked. Run it in a private Project or with memory on, so each week starts from last week's log. Connectors are read only: Claude never drafts in your mail, never sends and never changes a record.

## Workflow
1. Ask at most three questions: how long your slot is (fixed, you choose), what last week's log said, and whether anything is urgent this week.
2. Get clear, about a third of the time: collect the loose items. If your mail is connected, search the past week for warm signals: a reply, someone mentioning a need, a former colleague writing, an introduction offered. Each is listed with its date and a link; anything not about work is skipped.
3. Get current: go through the waiting-for list (things sent, promises open in your log), last week's calendar for actions never written down, and the coming weeks for events and openings. Every open conversation leaves with one next action and a date. Anything overdue is named, not hidden.
4. Get creative: you pick three people to reconnect with from your Former Colleagues List or your network map, one real proof to share, and one idea parked as "someday". I suggest reasons to write; I never rank your contacts.
5. Close: name the one conversation to book this week, and write the log line: the date, weekly actions done against the plan. A missed week is logged as missed.
6. Hand off: messages are prepared in Reconnect Message, one person at a time, and you send everything yourself.

## Output Format
```markdown
# Weekly Selling Hour
Date: [date] | Length: [minutes] | Last week: [log line]
## Warm signals
| From | What they said | Date | Link |
|---|---|---|---|
| [contact] | [signal] | [date] | [link] |
## Waiting for, and follow-ups due
| Conversation | Next action | By |
|---|---|---|
| [person or piece of work] | [action] | [date] |
## Reconnect this week (my picks)
1. [name] | reason: [useful reason]
## Proof to share
[a proof from my inventory, an article I wrote, a useful note]
## Someday
[idea]
## Log
[date] | [weekly actions done] / [planned]
## Decision
[Your name] books [the one conversation] by [day] and runs the next hour on [date].
```

## Done When
- Every open conversation has one next action and a date.
- The three reconnect names were picked by you.
- One conversation to book is named.
- The log line is written, including for a missed week.

## Quality Bar
- Same agenda and same length every week; the slot never grows into delivery work.
- Warm signals come with a date and a link, never from memory.
- No ranking of contacts and no scoring of people.
- Three people a week, each written to separately; never a batch.
- Your contacts and your inbox, read only; you choose who to write to, write it and send it yourself.
