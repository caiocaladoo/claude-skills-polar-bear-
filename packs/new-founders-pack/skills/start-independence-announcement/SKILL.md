# Independence Announcement

## When To Use
You have told nobody you are on your own, or you posted once and nothing happened. Most announcements say "excited to start a new chapter" and give a reader nothing to do. This answers: what do people who know you need to hear, once, so they can send the right person your way?

## When Not To Use
If you cannot yet say who you help in one line, run Positioning Statement first. To write to one person you have not spoken to in years, the announcement is the wrong tool; use Reconnect Message.

## Inputs
- Your Positioning Statement line and one proof from your Career Proof Inventory marked "may say in public"
- How you left, in one sentence you are comfortable saying in public
- Your Writing Voice Guide, if you have one, and any earlier announcement you posted
If you have none of this, I start from what you do now and who it is for, in your words, and mark the points as a first draft.

## Approach
An announcement that works has a simple structure, common practice with no single originator: what you now do, for whom, and one specific thing a reader can do. It is news plus a clear ask, not a launch. The judgement is restraint. The failure it prevents runs two ways: the post that reads as a job search ("open to opportunities"), and the post that reads as a firm with clients it does not have. One post rarely fills a calendar; it gives the people you write to next a reason to recognise your name.

## Workflow
1. Ask at most three questions: who you most want to read this (former colleagues, former clients, your wider network), what a useful introduction would look like, and whether your exit terms limit what you may say about your former employer or its clients.
2. Give the four points, each two or three sentences for you to write from: what you now do (your line), for whom (a role in a situation), what a useful introduction looks like ("if you know a [role] who is facing [situation]"), and one proof in public wording.
3. Give the do-not-claim list for this draft: clients you do not have, a former employer's results presented as yours, "fully booked", "we", urgency, and any thanks or criticism of the former employer that your terms or your judgement rule out.
4. You write the post in your own words. I do not write it.
5. Give points for the shorter version: a direct message of a few lines for the people who should hear it from you, not from a feed. Each one is sent by you, to people you choose, with a first line only you could write to that person.
6. On request, check what you wrote for grammar only and list each change. Then check it against the do-not-claim list and flag lines, without rewording them.
7. Note what comes after: the post is the start of the reconnects, so you set the date you begin them.

## Output Format
```markdown
# Independence Announcement
Audience: [who I most want to read this] | Limits from my exit terms: [none / what]
## Points for the public post
1. What I now do: [points]
2. For whom: [role in a situation]
3. A useful introduction looks like: [role] who is facing [situation]
4. One proof (inventory number [n], public wording): [points]
## Not in this post
- [claim to leave out, and why]
## My post (my words)
[your text]
## Points for the direct message version
- [what changes when it goes to one person]
## Checks on what I wrote
| Line | Grammar change, or flag from the not-in-this-post list |
|---|---|
| [line] | [change / flag] |
## Decision
[Your name] posts it from their own account on [date], chooses who gets the direct message, and starts the reconnects on [date].
```

## Done When
- The four points are filled from your positioning and your inventory.
- The post is in your words, and every grammar change is listed.
- Nothing on the do-not-claim list remains in the text.
- A date to start the reconnects is set.

## Quality Bar
- One specific ask, a role in a situation; never "let me know if you hear of anything".
- No hashtags stuffed in, no tagging of people to borrow their reach.
- The direct message is never pasted to a list; each goes to one person you chose.
- What you may say about a former employer or its clients: check with a qualified adviser.
- Your news in your own words, posted by you; nothing is invented, automated or sent for you.
