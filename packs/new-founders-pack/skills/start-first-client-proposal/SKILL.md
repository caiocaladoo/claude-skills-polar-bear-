# First Client Proposal

## When To Use
They asked for a proposal, and you have never written one that carries your own name and price. Inside a firm, a template, a rate card and a legal team stood behind you. This gives you a short document the person recognises as their own problem, with a clear answer and a price you worked out yourself.

## When Not To Use
If the call is still only in your head, run Discovery Call Recap first and wait for their corrections. If your standard offer fits as it is, send the One-Page Offer with a date to talk; a custom proposal is for when it does not fit.

## Inputs
- Your confirmed Discovery Call Recap, or your call notes and their message
- Your floor per day, your estimate of days by phase, and the price you are considering
- One proof from your Career Proof Inventory marked "may say in public"
If you have none of this, I start from your call notes alone and mark the output as a first draft with every gap in [brackets].

## Approach
The opening follows situation, complication, question, from Barbara Minto's work on structured writing: what they agree is true, what changed, the question they need answered, then your answer. The price section uses good, better, best options, described generically and with the tricks taken out: one to three options that are each a real answer, no decoy, no deadline. For a first client the judgement is to keep it short and walk them through it live. The failure it prevents is the twelve-page document, written to look like a firm, that buries the answer and is never opened twice.

## Workflow
1. Ask at most three questions: what they said they need answered, in their words; who reads this beyond the person you met; and when you will walk them through it.
2. Write the opening. Situation is only what they would nod at; complication is the change that makes it matter now; the question is theirs, not your service. Then your answer in two sentences.
3. Write their objective in their words from the recap, and the approach in two to four phases, each with what they see at the end and what you need from them. Add one line on what this work will not solve.
4. Decide how many options exist. Each must be a distinct answer you would gladly deliver at that price, and they must be able to say in one sentence what B adds over A. Options differ in scope and outcome, never only in price. If only one exists, one option is the honest proposal.
5. Set the fee per option from your numbers: days by phase x your day price, plus costs. I show the floor check beside it for you only (days x floor per day); the floor never reaches the client copy. I never suggest the price.
6. List five to eight assumptions that would change the number if wrong: their availability, the state of existing material, review rounds, the start date.
7. Write terms in plain words (validity, payment, what happens if scope changes) and one next step with a date and an owner on each side. Anything beyond plain commercial terms: check with a qualified adviser. Then cut until it fits one or two pages.

## Output Format
```markdown
# First Client Proposal
For: [name, role] | Prepared by: [me] | Version: [draft until I approve] | Walk-through: [date]
## Where you are and what you need answered
[Situation] [Complication] [Question] [My answer in two sentences]
Your objective, in your words: [from the recap of [date]]
## How we would work
| Phase | What happens | What you see at the end | What I need from you |
|---|---|---|---|
| [phase] | [activities] | [deliverable or decision] | [input, time] |
Not in scope: [what this work will not solve]
## Options
| Option | What you get | What it adds over the one before | Fee |
|---|---|---|---|
| [A] | [scope, outcome] | [n/a] | [my figure] |
| [B] | [scope, outcome] | [one sentence] | [my figure] |
## Assumptions the fee rests on
1. [assumption that would change the number]
## Terms and next step
- [validity, payment, scope change, in plain words] | Next step: [what], [date], [owner on each side]
## Internal notes (never in the client copy)
- Floor check per option: [days] x [floor per day] = [minimum] | Scope to remove if asked for less: [items]
## Decision
[Your name] approves every figure and sends it only after reading each line; [their name] chooses an option at the walk-through on [date].
```

## Done When
- Each option passes the one-sentence difference test.
- Every figure comes from your numbers and sits at or above your floor.
- The answer follows the question on page one, there is one dated next step, and it fits two pages.

## Quality Bar
- No option you would be unhappy to deliver; no decoy, anchor or "valid until Friday".
- This is a short proposal, not a contract; the agreement itself goes to a qualified adviser.
- Their words, your numbers and your own proof, with no result or quote from anywhere else; you approve it and send it yourself.
