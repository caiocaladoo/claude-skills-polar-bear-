# Monthly Practice Review

## When To Use
Three months in, one client pays every bill, and you have not looked at what happens when that contract ends. The work is going well, which is exactly why nobody looks. Once a month this answers: where did the money come from, how much rests on one client, how long is the runway now, and what do I change?

## When Not To Use
For what to do this week, run Weekly Selling Hour; this review looks back over a month and changes the plan, it does not fill the diary. In the first month, before any income, the checkpoints in 90-Day Practice Plan are enough.

## Inputs
- The month's invoices and payments, and your current bank balance for the practice, from your own records
- Your runway plan, your 90-day plan with its scoreboard, and your Conversation Log
- The limit you set for one client's share of income, if you set one
If you have none of this, I start from the month's income by client as you remember it and mark the output as a first draft.

## Approach
The frame is the After-Action Review, as USAID's 2006 technical guidance sets it out from the US Army's practice: what was intended, what actually happened, what went well and why, and what can be improved and how. It is a review of events, not a verdict on a person, and here the person is you. It is run on your numbers, with one check added because it is the first-year trap: the share of income held by your largest client. The failure it prevents is finding out you had one client and no pipeline on the day the contract ends.

## Workflow
1. Ask at most three questions: which month, what you intended at its start (from your plan), and whether you have set a limit for one client's share.
2. Lay out income by client and by source (former employer, former colleague, introduction, wider network, other), invoiced and paid, from your records. Late payments are listed with their dates.
3. Work out the largest client's share: that client's income / total income for the month and for the last three months. Show it beside the limit you set. If you set none, I ask for one and suggest no figure.
4. Update the runway: money available now / monthly burn = months left, and the date the largest contract ends. If the contract ends before a new one could start, the sheet says so plainly.
5. Compare the scoreboard with the plan: weekly actions done, conversations started, and where they came from. Counts of your actions only.
6. Ask the four questions and write your answers: what was intended, what happened, what went well and why, what to improve and how. Causes are choices and conditions, never your character.
7. End with one thing to stop and one thing to change, each with a date, and any change to the 90-day plan.

## Output Format
```markdown
# Monthly Practice Review
Month: [month] | Prepared: [date] | Records used: [invoices, statements]
## Income
| Client | Source | Invoiced | Paid | Late since |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| [client] | [former employer / former colleague / introduction / network / other] | [amount] | [amount] | [date or none] |
## Largest client
- Share this month: [client income] / [total] = [share] | Last three months: [share] | My limit: [limit I set]
- That contract ends: [date] | Work signed after it: [what, or none]
## Runway
[Money available] / [monthly burn] = [months], to [month, year]
## Plan against actual
| Weekly action | Planned | Done | Conversations it started |
|---|---|---|---|
| [action] | [number] | [number] | [number] |
## Review
- Intended: [from my plan] | Happened: [facts]
- Went well, and why: [answer] | To improve, and how: [answer]
## Decision
[Your name] stops [one thing] and changes [one thing] by [date], and updates the 90-day plan if the largest client's share is over the limit.
```

## Done When
- Every income figure comes from an invoice or a statement.
- The largest client's share is shown beside a limit you set.
- Runway is recalculated with the arithmetic visible.
- One stop and one change are named, each with a date.

## Quality Bar
- No benchmark for a "healthy" share, income or number of clients; the limit is yours.
- Clients are compared by income and source only; nobody is rated.
- A good month is reviewed with the same questions as a bad one.
- Tax and set-aside figures: check with a qualified adviser.
- Your own numbers from your own records; Claude does the arithmetic and you decide what changes.
