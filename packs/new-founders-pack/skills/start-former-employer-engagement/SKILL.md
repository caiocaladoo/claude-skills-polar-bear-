# Former Employer Engagement Plan

## When To Use
Your first client is your old employer, and it is starting to feel like your old job without the benefits. It is a common first engagement and a good one, until requests arrive from every direction, the end date slides, and the whole practice is one client. This answers: on what terms do I do this so that it stays a client and I stay a business?

## When Not To Use
If the question is what your old contract stops you doing elsewhere, run Former Employer Terms Check. If the client is new to you, a First Client Onboarding Note is enough.

## Inputs
- What they have asked you to do, by whom, for how long, and what they have said about pay
- Your floor per day and your runway plan
- Your old job description or a plain list of what you used to do there
If you have none of this, I start from their request as you remember it and mark the output as a first draft.

## Approach
Two plain practices, described generically: boundaries written before the work starts, and a limit on how much of your income one client holds. Beside them sits a question you cannot answer alone. Where one person works mainly for one organisation, authorities may ask whether that person is really independent. The US tax authority weighs behavioural control, financial control and the type of relationship; UK guidance covers people who supply services through an intermediary. Rules differ by country and the facts decide, so this plan turns them into questions. The failure it prevents: months in, you have your old desk, your old meetings, one client and no pipeline.

## Workflow
1. Ask at most three questions: what exactly they asked for and who asked, how long they expect it to run, and what share of your working week it would take.
2. Write the scope as deliverables or outcomes, never "support" or "cover", with an end date and a review date before it. Open-ended work gets a first term and a decision point.
3. Name one person you report to and one channel for requests. Requests from anyone else go through that person; write the sentence you will use when a former colleague asks "can you just".
4. Set the rate against your floor with the arithmetic shown. Your old salary is not the reference: you now carry your own costs, gaps and set-asides. I show the floor check; you set the rate.
5. Work out the share of your income and of your week this client would take, from your own figures, and write the limit you set and the date by which you want to be under it. I suggest no threshold.
6. Fill the "old job / this engagement" table: who directs the work, hours and place, tools and access, meetings attended, who you speak for. Each row that looks the same as before is flagged for the adviser.
7. Write the questions for a qualified adviser on worker status, tax and insurance where you live, and what to bring. I give no view on your status.

## Output Format
```markdown
# Former Employer Engagement Plan
Client: [former employer] | Asked by: [role] | Proposed term: [start] to [end] | Review on: [date]
## Scope
| Deliverable or outcome | Due | Not included |
|---|---|---|
| [deliverable] | [date] | [what stays out] |
## How requests reach me
- I report to: [one role] | Channel: [one channel] | My line for "can you just": [my words]
## Rate and share
- Rate: [my figure] | Floor check: [floor per day] | Gap: [figure]
- Share of my income: [share] | Share of my week: [days] | My limit: [limit I set], to be under it by [date]
## Old job / this engagement
| | Old job | This engagement | Same? |
|---|---|---|---|
| Who directs how I do the work | [then] | [now] | [yes: ask adviser / no] |
| Hours, place, tools, access | [then] | [now] | [yes: ask adviser / no] |
| Meetings, team rituals, speaking for the organisation | [then] | [now] | [yes: ask adviser / no] |
## Questions for a qualified adviser
1. [question on worker status, tax or insurance, and what I will bring]
## Decision
[Your name] sets the rate, the end date and the income limit by [date], and takes the questions to a qualified adviser before signing.
```

## Done When
- The scope is deliverables with an end date and a review date.
- One reporting line and one channel are named.
- The income and time shares are worked from your figures, with your own limit.
- Every "same as before" row is on the adviser's question list.

## Quality Bar
- No sentence says you are, or are not, an employee or a contractor, and no threshold for one client's share is supplied.
- Former colleagues appear as roles in a process, never as people to manage around.
- Nothing from the employer's systems is reused outside this engagement.
- Your numbers and your limits; you decide the terms, and worker status, tax and contract points go to an adviser: check with a qualified adviser.
