# LinkedIn Data Export Guide

## When To Use
You want to see your whole network in one list without a tool that logs in as you. Years of connections sit behind a search box, and the tools that offer to pull them out for you break LinkedIn's rules and put your account at risk the month you need it most. This answers: how do I get my own data, which parts do I need, and where does it live while I use it?

## When Not To Use
If you already hold a clean connections file in a private place, go straight to Warm Network Map. If you only want the people who saw your work, Former Colleagues List starts from memory and needs no export.

## Inputs
- What you will read the list against: your two-line brief, or your Ideal Client Profile
- Whether you want a first guess at closeness later (that decides whether you need the messages file)
- Where you will work: one private chat with file upload, or a private Project
If you have none of this, I start from the request steps alone and mark the output as a first draft.

## Approach
Your own data, downloaded by you, following LinkedIn Help's page on downloading your account data. A second LinkedIn Help page, on prohibited software and extensions, rules out tools that scrape or automate the site, which is why nothing in this guide logs in as you. The judgement is in what you leave out: the export holds other people's names, sometimes their email addresses, and in the messages file their own words. The failure it prevents: a full archive dropped into a shared folder and forgotten there.

## Workflow
1. Ask at most three questions: do you want closeness later, will you work in one private chat or a private Project, and on what date will you delete the copy.
2. Give the request steps as LinkedIn Help states them today: open the page, follow its menu path to the data download, and choose specific categories, not the full archive. Menus and delivery times change, so the plan links the help page and you note the date you asked.
3. Choose the files: the connections file always. For closeness, two options, your choice: upload the messages file to your private space, or count exchanges per person yourself and bring only the count. File names are checked in your own download, not guessed.
4. Leave the rest out: every category the method does not need. Fewer files means less of other people's data in your hands.
5. Clean the columns: keep name, title, organisation and the date you connected. Drop email addresses unless you want them in your own private copy. Some will be missing because people chose not to share them; you do not look for them elsewhere.
6. Check for work accounts: if your profile was tied to a former employer's tools, the export is still your own connections, but lists your employer gave you are not yours and stay out.
7. Decide where it lives: your private chat or private Project only, never a shared space. You put the deletion date in your own calendar.

## Output Format
```markdown
# LinkedIn Export Plan
Requested on: [date] | Help page followed: [link, date read] | Delete by: [date I set]
## Files
| File | Use it? | Why | Kept where |
|---|---|---|---|
| Connections file | Yes | Read against my brief | [private chat / private Project] |
| Messages file, or my own count | [my choice] | First guess at closeness | [private space only] |
| Everything else | No | Not needed | Not uploaded |
## Column cleanup
| Keep | Drop |
|---|---|
| Name, title, organisation, connected on | [emails, unless kept privately], [other columns] |
## Rules for this copy
- No scraping tool, bot or extension; no tool logs in as me
- Nothing from a former employer's lists
- Never shared; deleted on [date]
## Decision
[Your name] decides by [date] which files to upload and the deletion date, then requests the export personally.
```

## Done When
- The steps point to LinkedIn Help and the categories are chosen, not the full archive by default.
- Closeness is planned one of two ways, and you chose which.
- A private location and a deletion date are written down.

## Quality Bar
- Steps come from LinkedIn's own help page; nothing about menus, timings or file names is guessed.
- Missing email addresses stay missing; no lookup elsewhere.
- Questions about holding other people's data: check with a qualified adviser.
- The plan covers the download only; reading the names is the next skill.
- Your own data, downloaded by you and kept private; nothing is scraped and no tool logs in as you.
