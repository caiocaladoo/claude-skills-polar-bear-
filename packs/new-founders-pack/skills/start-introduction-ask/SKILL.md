# Introduction Ask

## When To Use
Asking former colleagues for help feels like begging, and "let me know if you hear of anything" has produced nothing. Use this when someone who knows your work has just been glad to hear from you, and you want to ask for one introduction without making it awkward. It answers: what exactly do I ask for, when, and how do I make no an easy answer?

## When Not To Use
If you want to meet one named person through someone who knows them, use Warm Introduction Request. If you have not been in touch for years, do not open with an ask; write a Reconnect Message first.

## Inputs
- Your Positioning Statement line and the "to meet" line of your brief: roles in situations
- The person you want to ask, how they know your work, and what just happened between you
- Something you can offer them, however small: an introduction, a reading, an hour of your experience
If you have none of this, I start from who you would most like to meet and mark the output as a first draft.

## Approach
Research from the Hinge Research Institute found that asking alone rarely produces introductions, and that people introduce in order to help the person with the problem, not the person selling. So the vague ask is out. What remains is practitioner practice: one specific introduction that clearly serves the person introduced, asked at a natural moment, with an easy way to decline. The failure it prevents is the end-of-call "if you hear of anyone", which costs your contact nothing to agree to and gives them nothing to act on.

## Workflow
1. Ask at most three questions: who you are asking, what just happened between you, and what one introduction would help most right now.
2. Check the moment. Good: right after they said something kind about your work, offered help, or a conversation went well. Not now: the first note in years, a strained relationship, a favour still owed. If the moment is wrong, the output says "wait" and names what would make it right.
3. Build one ask: "Who do you know who is a [role] and is facing [situation]?", or a person they named themselves. Never "anyone". A second ask waits for another day.
4. Write why it serves the person introduced: what they might gain from one conversation, from their side. If you cannot say it without mentioning your need, the ask is not ready.
5. Prompt the easy no: one line, in your words, that makes declining comfortable. The ask must survive a no with the relationship intact.
6. Choose what you offer in return, with no condition attached. No fee or reward by default; any such arrangement is a question for a qualified adviser.
7. List what not to say: pressure, scarcity, "I need work", praise used as a set-up, a second chase if no name comes back. You say or write it in your own words.

## Output Format
```markdown
# Introduction Ask
Person asked: [name] | How they know my work: [worked together on what] | Moment: [what just happened]
Ready: [ask now / wait until: condition]
## The ask
- I would like to meet: a [role] who is facing [situation]
- Why it serves them: [from their side, in my words]
- My words: [how I will put it]
## The easy no
- [my line that makes declining comfortable]
## What I offer
- [what, with no condition]
## What I will not say
- [pressure / scarcity / "I need work" / praise as set-up / chasing]
## If they say yes
- Thank-you: [when, how] | Tell them what happened: [by when]
## Decision
[Your name] decides whether to ask [name] now or wait, and says it in their own words by [date].
```

## Done When
- There is exactly one ask, a role in a situation or a person they named.
- The moment check says "ask now" or "wait", with the condition.
- The easy no and the offer are in your words.
- Nothing in the output is a finished message to paste.

## Quality Bar
- "Anyone" never appears as the ask.
- Why it serves the person introduced is written before anything about you.
- Nothing assesses how likely the person is to introduce you; nobody is rated.
- One ask per person per moment; no follow-up chasing a name.
- One specific ask, easy to decline, in your own words; you choose who to ask and say it yourself.
