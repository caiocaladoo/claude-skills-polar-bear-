# LinkedIn Headline

## When To Use
Your headline still says your last job title, or "Founder" and nothing else. Either way, someone who hears your name and opens your profile learns nothing about who you help now. This answers one question: what single line makes the right person keep reading?

## When Not To Use
If you cannot yet say what you do in one sentence, run Positioning Statement first; a headline cut from a vague sentence stays vague. If the headline works and the page under it reads like a CV, go to LinkedIn About Section.

## Inputs
- Your Positioning Statement: the line and its variants
- Your current headline, pasted as it reads today
- The words the people you served used for the problem, and one proof from your Career Proof Inventory marked "may say in public"
If you have none of this, I start from your current headline and one piece of past work, and mark the output as a first draft.

## Approach
LinkedIn Help's page on creating a good profile says the headline is filled in from your positions by default and can be rewritten to show an area of expertise. Here the expertise is said in three parts: who you help, the outcome, one real proof. The judgement is in the first few words, because only the start of a headline shows beside your comments and in search results. The failure it prevents: "Founder | Consultant | Adviser | Speaker", which could belong to anyone and tells a former colleague nothing they can pass on.

## Workflow
1. Ask at most three questions: which variant of your line you lean towards, which proof you are allowed to say in public, and whether you want your former role visible in the headline at all.
2. Pull the buyer words: the nouns and verbs people used when they came to you with the problem. Each gets its source and a mark, "theirs" or "mine". Their words win over trade terms.
3. Fill three parts for each option: who you help (a role in a situation), the outcome, and one proof. If no proof may be said in public, the slot stays empty; nothing is invented to fill it.
4. Write five options that differ on purpose: one leads with the who, one with the outcome, one with the proof, one in plain speech, one closest to how you now introduce yourself. The meaning sits in the opening words of each.
5. Decide the old title on purpose: a former role may appear as proof ("former [role]") if it is true, yours to state and helps a buyer; it never leads.
6. Count each option against the limit LinkedIn's editor shows you; the limit can change, so check the counter in your own editor. Flag any option that needs its end to make sense.
7. Read each aloud as an answer to "so what do you do now?" Strike any you would not say to a former boss's face. You pick one and paste it yourself.

## Output Format
```markdown
# LinkedIn Headline Options
Current headline: [pasted as it reads today]
## Buyer words
| Word or phrase | Where it came from | Theirs or mine |
|---|---|---|
| [word] | [conversation, note, past work] | [theirs / mine] |
## Options
| # | Headline | Leads with | Proof (inventory number) | Characters | Read-aloud verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | [option] | [who / outcome / proof / plain / introduction] | [number or "none"] | [count] | [keep / strike] |
## Words dropped
- [word everyone in my field uses, and why it goes]
## Decision
[Your name] chooses the headline and pastes it into the profile by [date]; the runner-up is kept to try on [date].
```

## Done When
- Five options, each within the limit your editor shows, each with its meaning in the opening words.
- Every proof is real and marked "may say in public", or the slot says "none".
- Buyer words are marked as theirs or yours.
- You have read each option aloud and picked one.

## Quality Bar
- No string of services separated by bars.
- Who you help is a role in a situation, never a named person or organisation.
- A former employer is named only if that is true, public and allowed by your terms.
- "Founder" alone is never the headline.
- The proof is real and yours to state; Claude drafts options, you choose and edit the profile yourself.
