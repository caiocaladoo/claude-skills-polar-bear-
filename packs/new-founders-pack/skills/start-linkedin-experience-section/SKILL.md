# LinkedIn Experience Section

## When To Use
Your profile lists twenty years of job titles and no entry for the business you run today. A buyer scrolling down sees an employee who stopped. This answers: what goes at the top for the practice, and how does each past role read as evidence that you have solved this kind of problem before?

## When Not To Use
If you have not listed what you may say about your past work, run Career Proof Inventory first; this skill only writes lines from rows already marked. For the text above the entries, use LinkedIn About Section.

## Inputs
- Your current experience entries, pasted as they read today
- Your Career Proof Inventory with its "may say" marks, and your Positioning Statement
- The name, start date and one-line description of your practice
If you have none of this, I start from your two most recent roles as pasted and mark the output as a first draft.

## Approach
LinkedIn Help's page on creating a good profile advises including the experience that fits your current goals. Your goal changed: the reader is now a buyer, not a recruiter. So each past role is cut to two or three lines in the challenge, action, result form, chosen for today's client, and the practice gets a real entry of its own. The failure it prevents: the duty list ("responsible for a team of...") that proves you held a job and says nothing about what you can do for the person reading.

## Workflow
1. Ask at most three questions: what your practice is called and when it started, which past roles matter most to the clients you want, and whether any employer or client must not be described at all.
2. Write the current entry first: your real title (founder, consultant, adviser; no inflated one), who it is for, what you do in two lines, and one plain way to reach you. It is dated from the true start and makes no claim of clients you do not have.
3. For each past role, pick two or three rows from your inventory marked "may say in public" that matter to today's buyer. Write each as one line: the challenge, what you did, what changed. Titles, employers and dates stay exactly true.
4. Run the confidentiality check per line: does it name a client, quote a figure that was never public, or describe something your terms cover? Each line is marked clear, reword or remove, with the reason. Doubt means remove.
5. Build the remove list: duty lists, internal programme names and jargon, awards a buyer cannot read, and old roles that add nothing for this reader (shortened to title and dates, not deleted, so the history stays honest).
6. Read the section top to bottom as a buyer: does the story run from "this is what I do now" to "this is why you can believe it"? You paste every entry yourself.

## Output Format
```markdown
# LinkedIn Experience Section
## Current entry
- Title: [my real title] at [practice name] | From: [true start date]
- Who it is for: [role in a situation]
- What I do: [two lines]
- Reach me: [one plain way]
## Past roles, reworded
| Role and employer (unchanged) | Line (challenge, action, result) | Inventory row | Confidentiality check |
|---|---|---|---|
| [title, employer, dates] | [one line] | [number] | [clear / reword: reason / remove: reason] |
## To remove or shorten
| Entry or line | Why | Remove or shorten |
|---|---|---|
| [duty list, internal jargon, old role] | [reason] | [action] |
## Decision
[Your name] approves each line marked clear, settles every "reword" by [date], and pastes the entries into the profile.
```

## Done When
- The practice has its own entry, dated truthfully, with who it is for and how to reach you.
- Every past-role line comes from an inventory row marked "may say in public".
- Every line carries a confidentiality mark with its reason.
- No title, employer or date has been changed.

## Quality Bar
- No inflated title and no "clients include" line for clients of a former employer.
- A result is stated only if it is yours to state; otherwise the line says what you did.
- Old roles are shortened, never erased or re-dated.
- Whether your terms allow a line is a legal question: check with a qualified adviser.
- Lines come from your own career, never a former employer's documents; you approve and paste every one.
