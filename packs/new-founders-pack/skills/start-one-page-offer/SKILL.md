# One-Page Offer

## When To Use
Someone says "send me something I can pass on", and all you have is a CV. A CV asks to be hired; a contact who wants to help needs a page that tells a third person, in a minute, whether to call you. This answers: what does that page say?

## When Not To Use
If the offer itself is not defined (scope, exclusions, a price you have checked), run Productized Service Offer first; this page presents an offer, it does not invent one. For one named client with a specific problem, write a First Client Proposal.

## Inputs
- Your Productized Service Offer: outcome, scope, timeline, first step
- Your Positioning Statement line, and the problem in the words people use for it
- One proof from your Career Proof Inventory marked "may say in public", and how you want to be contacted
If you have none of this, I start from your description of the offer in a few lines and mark the page as a first draft.

## Approach
The page follows Barbara Minto's pyramid principle: the point first, then what supports it. A forwarded page is read without you in the room, by someone who did not ask for it, so the first two lines must say who it is for and what they get. The judgement is in what stays off: your history, your method's name, every other thing you could do. The failure it prevents is the brochure of six services, which a contact will not forward because they cannot tell who it is meant for. The page can be written in Claude Docs and edited in place, or exported as a PDF through file creation.

## Workflow
1. Ask at most three questions: who will forward this and to what kind of person, whether the price appears on the page (your choice), and how you want to be contacted.
2. Write the top two lines: who it is for (a role in a situation) and the outcome they get. Test them alone, as if nothing else were read.
3. Write the problem in their words, three or four lines at most, from your notes. No statistics about the market; nothing you cannot source to your own experience.
4. Write the offer: what happens, in three to five steps, what they hold at the end, how long it takes, and what you need from them. The exclusions stay in your internal offer; one line says what this is not.
5. Add one proof in public wording, saying plainly it comes from a previous role, and one line on who you are. No logos of a former employer's clients.
6. Write how to start: the small first step and one way to reach you. If you chose to show the price, it is the one you set in your offer; your floor and price working never appear.
7. Run the forward test: read it as the third person. Can they tell in a minute who it is for, what they get and what to do? Then check it fits one page.

## Output Format
```markdown
# One-Page Offer
## [Offer name]
**For:** [role in a situation] | **You get:** [outcome, one sentence]
## The problem
[Three or four lines in their words]
## What happens
1. [step] | 2. [step] | 3. [step]
- At the end you have: [what they hold]
- It takes: [weeks] | From you: [access, people, decisions]
- This is not: [one line]
## Proof and who I am
[One proof, public wording, from a previous role] | [one line on me]
## How to start
[The first step] | [price, only if I chose to show it] | [one way to reach me]
## Forward test
| Is this for me? | What do I get? | What do I do next? |
|---|---|---|
| [line that answers it] | [line] | [line] |
## Decision
[Your name] approves the page, decides whether the price is shown, and sends it to [first contact] by [date].
```

## Done When
- The top two lines say who it is for and what they get.
- Every line of the offer matches your Productized Service Offer.
- The proof is marked "may say in public" in your inventory.
- The forward test passes and the page fits one page.

## Quality Bar
- One offer on the page; a second offer gets its own page.
- No claim the forwarding contact would have to vouch for and cannot check.
- No internal figures: floor, days and margin stay in your own notes.
- No invented client names, results or testimonials; a missing proof is a bracket.
- Your offer, your proof, your price; you approve the page and send it yourself.
