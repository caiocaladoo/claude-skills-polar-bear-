# Price Conversation Prep

## When To Use
The price conversation is tomorrow, and you already know you will offer a discount before they ask. You have negotiated budgets for an employer for years; saying your own price aloud for your own work is a different thing. This sheet answers, before the call: what is my number, where do I stop, and what will I trade that is not the price?

## When Not To Use
If you have no number yet, finish First Client Proposal first. If you do not know your floor, run Floor Rate Calculator; without it there is nothing to hold.

## Inputs
- Your proposal with the number you will put forward, your floor per day and the delivery days for this work
- Your runway plan and what else is in conversation
- What they have said about budget, timing and who decides, in their words
If you have none of this, I start from your number and your floor alone and mark the output as a first draft.

## Approach
BATNA, the best alternative to a negotiated agreement, as the Harvard Program on Negotiation explains it: look at the options you could really pursue, not imagined ones, and put the alternative and the offer on the same terms. Beside it sits a second plain idea, looking for the interests behind what the other side says. A new founder's alternative is often thin: no other client yet, and a number of months of runway. It is written down honestly, because a weak alternative changes how much you concede, not what you say. The failure it prevents is the price that falls in the first minute, to answer a "no" nobody said.

## Workflow
1. Ask, all at once: what number are you putting forward and from which document, what happens to your next three months if this does not close, and what have they said about budget in their words?
2. Write your number and your floor side by side. The floor for this work is delivery days x floor per day; it is internal and never appears in anything they see.
3. Write your alternative if there is no deal, only what is real: other conversations and how far along they are, runway in months, time freed for selling. Compare it with this deal on the same terms: money, time, what it leads to. If it is weak, the sheet says so.
4. List their interests behind their likely position (spend, risk of trying someone new, timing, proving it to their boss), only from what they said. Add the questions that would teach you more. I never guess their budget.
5. Name what carries the price: the scope, the assumptions, the outcome they described. Then draft your first two sentences in your own voice: the number, then what it buys. No apology, no "I know it is a lot". Add one line for when they go quiet, which is nothing about price.
6. List what you can trade and what each costs you (scope, phasing, timing, payment terms), with something asked in return. Then the line you will not cross: the same work for less.

## Output Format
```markdown
# Price Conversation Prep Sheet
With: [name or code] | Call: [date] | Offer: [proposal, option]
## Numbers (internal)
| Item | Figure | Source |
|---|---|---|
| My number | [my figure] | [proposal] |
| My floor for this work | [days] x [floor per day] = [figure] | [floor sheet] |
| My alternative if no deal | [what is real, in money and time] | [runway plan, open conversations] |
## Their interests
| What I know (their words) | Interest behind it | Question to learn more |
|---|---|---|
| [as said] | [spend / risk / timing / proof] | [open question] |
## What I say
- What carries the price: [scope line, assumption, outcome they named]
- My first two sentences: [the number] [what it buys]
- If they go quiet: [my line, not about price]
## Trades
| I can offer | It costs me | I ask in return |
|---|---|---|
| [scope, phasing, timing, terms] | [cost] | [what I value] |
Not on the table: the same work for less.
## Decision
[Your name] decides before [call date] the lowest point they accept and which trades they offer, and has the conversation personally.
```

## Done When
- Number, floor and alternative are filled from your own records, or marked missing.
- The first two sentences state the number before any explanation.
- Each trade has a cost to you and something asked in return.

## Quality Bar
- The floor never leaves the internal section.
- No tactics: no anchoring, no silence tricks, no deadline pressure, no invented other bidder, no market rate used as a reason.
- The other person is described by interests, never by personality.
- Your numbers and your honest alternative; Claude prepares, and you have the conversation yourself.
