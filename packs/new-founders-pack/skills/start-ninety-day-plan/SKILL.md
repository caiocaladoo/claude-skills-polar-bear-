# 90-Day Practice Plan

## When To Use
You have never had to sell, and nobody is going to give you a target. For years the work arrived through an employer; now a week can pass in useful-feeling activity with no conversation started. This answers one question: what do I do every week, in the time I really have, so that paid work exists in 90 days?

## When Not To Use
If you already have a plan and the selling simply stops when delivery starts, run Weekly Selling Hour. If you do not yet know your runway or floor, run First-Year Runway Plan and Floor Rate Calculator first; the goal is worked back from them.

## Inputs
- Your runway plan (the month by which work must be signed), your floor per day and your offer's fee
- How many conversations you have open today, and where they came from
- The hours a week you can give to finding work, in your heaviest week
If you have none of this, I start from the goal alone, leave every other number as a [placeholder] and mark the plan as a first draft.

## Approach
The plan uses lead and lag measures from FranklinCovey's 4 Disciplines of Execution: one lag measure you want (the result), and two or three lead measures you control and can count every week. "Get three meetings a week" fails at once, because the other person decides; "write three reconnect messages a week" does not. Checkpoints at 30, 60 and 90 days are common practice. A new practice has no conversion history, so the plan does not pretend to one: those cells stay as placeholders and are filled from your own first weeks. Channels run warm first, because first clients tend to come from people who already know your work. The plan can live in Claude Docs or a private Project so you reopen it weekly.

## Workflow
1. Ask at most three questions: what must be true in 90 days (signed work, in your unit), the hours you can give in your worst week, and where your open conversations came from.
2. Set one lag measure and work it back with your figures: income needed by the runway date / your offer's fee = pieces of work needed. How many conversations that takes is unknown for you yet, so it stays "[to learn by day 30]". One goal, not five.
3. Lay out the channel order: former colleagues and people who saw your work, your wider warm network, introductions, then events. You set the share of time for each. Cold outreach at volume and platforms are not in this plan.
4. Choose two or three lead measures you control and can count weekly: reconnect messages written, introduction asks made, conversations held, one proof shared. Reject any that depends on someone else saying yes.
5. Run the capacity check: add up the weekly time the lead measures need and compare it with your worst-week hours. If it does not fit, cut a measure.
6. Set the three checkpoints. Day 30: are the weekly actions happening, and what do the first numbers say? Day 60: which channel started real conversations? Day 90: the lag measure against the goal. At each, you write what you will look at and what would make you change the plan.
7. Write the stop list (what you will stop doing to free the time) and set up the scoreboard the weekly hour fills in.

## Output Format
```markdown
# 90-Day Practice Plan
Dates: [start] to [day 90] | Hours a week in my heaviest week: [hours]
## Goal for 90 days (lag measure)
[Signed work] = [income needed by runway date] / [my offer's fee] | Conversations needed: [to learn by day 30]
## Channel mix
| Channel | Why it fits me | Share of my time |
|---|---|---|
| [people who saw my work / wider warm network / introductions / events] | [from my list or map] | [share] |
## Weekly actions I control (lead measures)
| Action | Weekly target I set | Time it takes |
|---|---|---|
| [action] | [number] | [minutes] |
## Checkpoints
| Day | What I look at | What would make me change the plan |
|---|---|---|
| 30 / 60 / 90 | [measure] | [my rule] |
## What I will stop
- [activity] to free [time]
## Scoreboard
| Week | [Lead 1] | [Lead 2] | [Lead 3] | Conversations held |
|---|---|---|---|---|
## Decision
[Your name] confirms the goal and the weekly actions by [date], and books the weekly hour and the three checkpoints in the calendar.
```

## Done When
- One goal, worked back from your own numbers, with the arithmetic shown.
- Every lead measure is an action you control and can count weekly.
- The weekly actions fit your worst-week hours, and three checkpoints are dated with what would change the plan.

## Quality Bar
- No conversion rate, benchmark or target appears unless you gave it; unknowns stay as placeholders.
- Warm channels come first, nothing is automated, and the plan counts your actions, never targets for named people.
- Every number is yours; Claude does the arithmetic and you set the goal and the weekly actions.
