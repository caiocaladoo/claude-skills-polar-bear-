# LinkedIn About Section

## When To Use
Your About was written to get you hired, and a buyer who clicks through finds a career history. It lists years of experience and ends with "open to opportunities". This answers: what does a buyer need to read here to decide that a conversation with you is worth their time?

## When Not To Use
If your headline still says only a job title, run LinkedIn Headline first; the About backs a promise, so the promise comes first. For the job entries below the About, use LinkedIn Experience Section.

## Inputs
- Your headline and your Positioning Statement
- Your current About, pasted
- Two proofs from your Career Proof Inventory marked "may say in public"
- Your Writing Voice Guide, or two pieces of writing that sound like you
If you have none of this, I start from your current About and one piece of past work, and mark the output as a first draft.

## Approach
LinkedIn Help's page on creating a good profile frames the About as one or two paragraphs on your goals, motivation and skills. That advice is written for people looking for a job. For a founder the reader is a buyer, so the text follows Barbara Minto's pyramid principle: the point first, then situation, complication and question. The opening two lines carry the point, because many readers never click "see more". The failure it prevents: a warm, well-written story of your career that never says who you help.

## Workflow
1. Ask at most three questions: who you most want to reach through this page, which two proofs you will use, and what you would like a reader to do after reading.
2. Write the opening two lines first and test them alone: who you help and the change they get, in your voice. If they would not make a buyer click "see more", rewrite them before anything else.
3. Build the body in order: the situation your buyer is in, the complication that makes it hard now, the question you answer. Buyer words come from your notes; any phrase I supply is marked for you to replace.
4. Add how you work in plain steps a buyer can picture, not a list of skills. One sentence on what you do not do, if you have a clear "not for".
5. Place the two proofs: what was happening, what you did, what changed, in the public wording from your inventory. Say plainly that it was in a previous role. No number unless it is yours to state and exact.
6. Add one honest line on being new: you started this practice on [date], and the experience behind it is [years, in what]. Readers trust that more than a profile pretending to be an established firm.
7. Close with one plain way to start a conversation, then check the length against the limit your editor shows and against your voice guide. You paste the final text yourself.

## Output Format
```markdown
# LinkedIn About Section
## Opening two lines
[Line one: who I help and the change] [Line two: why now, or what makes my way different]
## Body
[Situation the buyer is in] [Complication] [The question I answer] [How I work, in plain steps]
## Proof
| Proof (inventory number) | Public wording | Says it was in a previous role? |
|---|---|---|
| [number] | [what happened, what I did, what changed] | [yes] |
## New and honest
[Started this practice on [date]; the experience behind it]
## Start a conversation
[One plain line: what to write to me about, and where]
## Checks
| Check | Result |
|---|---|
| Characters against my editor's limit | [count] |
## Decision
[Your name] approves the final text and pastes it into the profile by [date].
```

## Done When
- The opening two lines make sense with nothing after them.
- The body follows situation, complication, question, then how you work.
- Both proofs are marked "may say in public" in your inventory.
- The text fits the limit your editor shows and ends with one plain way to talk.

## Quality Bar
- No closing list of skills or keywords; buyer words sit inside sentences.
- No "we" for a practice of one, and no borrowed client list.
- Anything about confidentiality or your old contract: check with a qualified adviser.
- Proofs come from your own career in words you may use; you paste it into your profile.
