# Positioning Statement

## When To Use
Former colleagues like you and still cannot say what you do now. When a friend of theirs has the problem, your name does not come up, because "she left and does consulting" is all they have. This answers: compared with what else the client could do, why you, in a line someone else can say?

## When Not To Use
If you are not yet sure who you are for, start with Ideal Client Profile. If you want the line on your profile, settle it here first and then run LinkedIn Headline.

## Inputs
- Your Ideal Client Profile, or a short note on the situation you help with
- Your three proofs from the Career Proof Inventory, in public wording
- How people you served described the problem, in their words
If you have none of this, I start from how you describe yourself today and two pieces of past work, and mark the output as a first draft.

## Approach
The five components of positioning, as April Dunford sets them out on her site: competitive alternatives, differentiated capabilities, value, the customers who care most, and the market category. You start from what the client would do if you did not exist. For a new founder that list is honest and uncomfortable: hire someone like you full time, ask another adviser, do nothing, or do it themselves with AI. Research from the Hinge Research Institute found buyers often rule out a firm they were introduced to because the service was unclear, so the statement ends in a line built for a third person to repeat.

## Workflow
1. Ask at most three questions: what would a client do if you were not available, which proof are you proudest of, and who are three people who know your work well enough to test a line on?
2. List the alternatives fairly, with what each does well: a full-time hire, another adviser or firm, doing nothing, doing it themselves, doing it themselves with AI.
3. Name the capabilities those alternatives lack: judgement from having lived the situation, a method, speed without a hiring process, independence. Attach a proof from your inventory to each, or mark it "needs proof".
4. Translate each capability into value in the client's terms: what becomes possible, faster, safer or decided. Drop any capability you cannot connect to value.
5. Say who cares most (the situation and the role in the decision), then choose a category people already understand.
6. Write the line on this pattern: "[Name] helps [role] who [situation] to [result in their words]." Then three variants that each change one thing only: the situation, the role or the result. List the words to drop: jargon, method names, your old job title, adjectives about yourself.
7. Set the repeat-back test: say one variant to each of the three people, ask them to say it back in their own words, and note what they kept and lost. The test is whether they can repeat it, not whether they like it.

## Output Format
```markdown
# Positioning Statement
## Alternatives
| What the client could do instead | What it does well | Where it falls short for them |
|---|---|---|
| [hire full time / another adviser / do nothing / do it ourselves / do it ourselves with AI] | [fair point] | [gap, in their terms] |
## Capabilities, proof and value
| Capability | Proof from my own work | Value it makes possible |
|---|---|---|
| [capability] | [proof number, or "needs proof"] | [in their terms] |
## Who cares most, and the category
[Situation and role] | [category frame, and why]
## The line
- Base: [Name] helps [role] who [situation] to [result].
- Variants: A [situation changed] | B [role changed] | C [result changed]
- Words to drop: [word] ([jargon / old title / self-praise])
## Repeat-back test
| Tested with | Variant | What they said back | Kept / lost |
|---|---|---|---|
| [someone who knows my work] | [A / B / C] | [their words] | [notes] |
## Decision
[Your name] picks the line after testing it on three people, by [date].
```

## Done When
- "Hire full time" and "do it ourselves with AI" are listed and treated fairly.
- Every capability has proof from your own work or says "needs proof".
- Each variant differs from the base in exactly one thing.
- The line fits in one breath and carries no old job title.

## Quality Bar
- Never sell against AI or against a full-time hire; say what each does well.
- Value is in the client's terms, never a list of your methods.
- No competitor and no former employer is named in the line.
- Proof comes from your own career in words you may use; you pick the line and say it your way.
