# Reconnect Message

## When To Use
The first time a former colleague hears from you in years is the week you need work, and you both know it. Run this for one person who knows you, when you want to write for a reason that is useful to them and not a pitch in disguise.

## When Not To Use
If the person you need is one step away through someone else, use Warm Introduction Request. If you are ready to ask someone who knows your work for an introduction, that is Introduction Ask, and it comes after a real exchange, not in the first note.

## Inputs
- Who you are writing to, how you worked together, and when you last spoke
- Your last thread with them, pasted from your own account, or found with the Gmail connector (search and read only)
- Any public link about their work; your Writing Voice Guide if you have one
If you have none of this, I start from their name, their role and your last contact as you remember it, and mark the points as a first draft.

## Approach
Levin, Walter and Murnighan's study of dormant ties (Organization Science, 2011) found that reconnecting with people you once knew well can bring trust and new information together. It works when the reason to write serves the other person and you give before you ask. For a new founder there is one extra line to handle: you have left, and they may not know. It is said once, plainly, as news. The failure it prevents: "Long time no speak! I have set up on my own, any projects coming up?", which tells them exactly why you wrote.

## Workflow
1. Ask at most three questions: who they are to you and when you last spoke, which reason to write fits (share, catch up, congratulate, introduce, suggest a conversation), and whether there is anything you should not mention.
2. Read the last thread and any public link. Facts come only from those: their role, a piece of their work, a change they announced. Nothing from their personal life and nothing guessed.
3. Write 2 or 3 talking points, each 3 or 4 sentences, each with its source (the link, or the date of the thread). A point with no source is cut.
4. Add the one-line news: what you do now, in your positioning line, with no offer attached. If your terms check marked this person "ask adviser first", I stop here and say so.
5. Keep it free of any ask for work. "Suggest a conversation" stays low key and easy to decline. No false warmth: "I was just thinking of you" only if true.
6. You write the message in your own words. On request I check it for grammar only and list each change; I do not reword it.
7. You set a follow-up date. One follow-up at most, and only if you have something useful to add.

## Output Format
```markdown
# Reconnect Message
To: [name], [role] | Worked together: [where, when] | Last contact: [date, how]
Reason to write: [share / catch up / congratulate / introduce / suggest a conversation]
## Talking points
1. [3 or 4 sentences] (source: [link or thread date])
2. [3 or 4 sentences] (source: [link or thread date])
## My news, in one line
[What I do now, no offer attached]
## My draft (my words)
[your message]
## Grammar changes
| Original | Corrected | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| [text] | [text] | [grammar point] |
## Follow-up
Date: [date] | Useful thing to add: [item]
## Decision
[Your name] sends the message from their own account by [date], and decides on [date] whether one follow-up is worth sending.
```

## Done When
- The reason to write was chosen by you from the five.
- Every talking point names its source.
- The draft is in your words, and grammar changes are listed one by one.
- A follow-up date is set, with one follow-up at most.

## Quality Bar
- No ask for work, no pitch, no sequence, no template reused for the next person.
- Public work facts and your own thread only; nothing personal.
- Checked against your Writing Voice Guide when you have one.
- One message, one person; a run is about 10 people, never everyone at once.
- Claude gives points with sources; you choose the person, write the message and send it yourself.
