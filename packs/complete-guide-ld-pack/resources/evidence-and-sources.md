# Evidence and sources

## What this guide claims

**It claims:** each skill applies a named method or produces a standard L&D artifact; each named method is attributed to its originator through a public page, an institution guide or a peer-reviewed paper; and the research below supports the design choices the skills make (retrieval practice in checks, spacing in follow-up, practice with feedback in role-play, managers in transfer, less extraneous material on screens and slides, a draft tried by real learners before release).

## Deliberate design choices

These are 90n's choices, not findings from the sources. The red line is ours: Claude drafts and checks, people decide: you talk to the learners, the experts and the sponsor, a named person signs off what gets built and what counts as proof, and nothing a learner does in training is used to grade, rank or build a case against them. So are the rules that follow from it. Needs are analysed at role and task level, never by rating named people, even though the needs analysis guidance includes an individual level. Task ratings describe tasks and are filled by people who do the work. The feedback form rates the programme and never the trainer. Role-play observer notes go only to the learner. Trainer preparation ends in a readiness conversation with no score. The follow-up on use selects nobody by how they perform: it is anonymous, conversations are with volunteers who gave written consent, and results are read by group with small groups suppressed. A learner walkthrough and a pilot test the draft and the programme, and participants are told so. Vendor selection scores proposals, never people. Nobody's use of AI is counted or monitored. Every recommendation ends with a decision a named person makes.

Three methods come from other fields and are adapted: interest-based negotiation for the meeting with a sponsor, thinking aloud testing (a usability method) for a draft course, and a public-spending business case model cut to one page for one programme. Two were written for the public sector (the business case model and the bid evaluation note) and are used only for their structure. Legal, regulatory, contract, accessibility-conformance, privacy and data protection points are never stated as advice: check with a qualified adviser. The guide names roles only, and "your LMS" and "your authoring tool" instead of products.
