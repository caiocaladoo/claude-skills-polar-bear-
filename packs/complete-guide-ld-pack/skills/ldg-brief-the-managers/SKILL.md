# Brief the Managers
## When To Use
Learners go back to a manager who never asks what they learned. Most managers are not against the programme; nobody told them what their part was, or made it small enough to fit a week that is already full. Use this for one programme, before it runs. It answers: what does the manager say, ask and remove, before and after, so people get to use it?

## When Not To Use
If you need the whole mix of assignments, coaching and courses for a role, run Build the 70-20-10 Plan. If you want to know weeks later whether the group used it, run Check Use on the Job: that reads across everyone for the sponsor, while the log here is what each manager notes as they go.

## Inputs
- The programme: its objectives or top actions, its dates, and who attends, by role
- One or two real tasks where the skill should show up at work, and what managers already have in the calendar: a one-to-one, a team meeting, a shift handover
If you have none of this, I start from the programme title and one objective and mark the output as a first draft.

## Approach
Transfer of training research (Blume, Ford, Baldwin and Huang, 2010): transfer relates to motivation and a supportive work environment; the evidence is correlational. The manager's part follows the required drivers of the Kirkpatrick model: reinforce, monitor, encourage, reward. The judgement is in keeping each ask small and inside a meeting that already happens. The failure this prevents: the learner comes back keen, finds the diary booked solid and the old way still the fast way, and by the time anyone asks, the handout is under a pile.

## Workflow
1. Ask up to three questions: which behaviour the manager should see first at work, what usually blocks it (time, tool, process, workload), and when managers already meet their people.
2. Write the note to managers: one paragraph, readable in a minute, saying what the programme is for, the two conversations asked of them, and that nothing here assesses anyone.
3. Write the before guide: a short conversation, its length set by you, three questions at most, in words a manager would say. Why this programme, what the person wants from it, which task they will try first.
4. Fix the first on-the-job task: real work that uses the skill, within a window you set. The manager clears room for it. If nothing can move, that is the first barrier, and it goes in the log.
5. Write the after guide: what did you try, what happened, what got in the way, what do you need from me. The manager listens and removes one obstacle. It is never a quiz or a demonstration on demand.
6. Place the check-ins inside existing meetings, each mapped to a driver: reinforce (a prompt or the job aid), monitor (ask about the task), encourage (name the effort), reward (visible credit for using it).
7. Draw the barrier log: date, category (time, tool, process, workload, other) and the condition. There is no field about the person. It goes to L&D and is read in aggregate, with groups under a size you set suppressed.

## Output Format
```markdown
# Manager Briefing Kit
Programme: [title] | Dates: [dates] | Behaviour to see first: [behaviour]
## Note to managers (one minute)
[One paragraph: what the programme is for, the two conversations, the first task, and that nothing here feeds a review]
## Before: [minutes] with each person
| Ask, in your own words | Listen for |
|---|---|
| [question] | [what to pick up] |

First task back at work: [real task] | Within: [window you set] | Room made by: [what the manager moves]
## After: the first conversation back
| Ask | Then do |
|---|---|
| [question] | [support given or obstacle removed] |
## Check-ins in meetings that already exist
| Date | Meeting | Driver | What the manager does |
|---|---|---|---|
| [date] | [one-to-one / team meeting] | [reinforce / monitor / encourage / reward] | [action] |
## Barrier log (conditions only)
| Date | Category | What blocked use | Sent to L&D |
|---|---|---|---|
| [date] | [time / tool / process / workload / other] | [the condition] | [yes / no] |
## Decision
[L&D lead name] and [sponsor name] agree who fixes which kind of barrier, and when the log is read, by [date].
```

## Done When
- The note reads in a minute and each guide fits one page, in words a manager would say
- The first task is real work with a window you set, and every check-in names a driver and sits in a meeting that already exists
- The barrier log has categories and no field that describes a person

## Quality Bar
- The transfer evidence is described as correlational; the kit promises no uplift
- Nothing asks the manager to test, rate or report on anyone
- A blocked first task is logged as a condition, with what would have to move; lengths, windows and dates are yours
- The kit helps a manager support learning; nothing in it feeds a performance review, and the barrier log records conditions, never people.
