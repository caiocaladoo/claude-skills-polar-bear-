# Prepare the Sponsor Conversation

## When To Use
The diagnosis says a course will not fix it and you have to tell the person who asked for the course. Use this the day before that meeting, when you have the findings and no plan for how to say them without sounding like you are refusing the work. It answers: how do I open, what do I put on the table, and what do we agree before we leave the room?

## When Not To Use
This skill does not re-diagnose. If you have no cause table yet, run Diagnose the Performance Gap first. If the fix is agreed and the question is money for a programme, that argument is Make the Business Case.

## Inputs
- The diagnosis: the problem line, the gap by task, the causes with their evidence and the recommendation.
- The request as the sponsor made it, what you know about what the sponsor answers for (a target, a deadline, an audit, a commitment made upward), and what you can offer and by when.
If you have none of this, I start from the request and your one-line finding and mark the output as a first draft.

## Approach
Interest-based negotiation, as the Program on Negotiation at Harvard Law School describes it on its public pages: separate the people from the problem, focus on interests not positions, generate options, and use objective criteria. Here it is applied to choosing a fix after a performance diagnosis; it is a method from negotiation, not from L&D, and it assumes the sponsor still has room to decide. The failure it prevents: you open with "training is not the answer", the sponsor hears "no", the course gets commissioned from someone else, and you are left out of the next request.

## Workflow
1. Ask at most three questions: what did the sponsor ask for and by when, what do they answer for that this request protects, and what can you offer that is not a course?
2. Open with the problem, apart from the people. The page starts with the shared problem line and the evidence, in the sponsor's own measure. It never starts with what was wrong with the request.
3. Separate position from interest. The position is what was asked for ("a course by [date]"). The interest is what the sponsor answers for: a number, a deadline, a risk, being seen to act. I propose likely interests about the role's goals, each marked "to confirm in the meeting", so you ask instead of assuming.
4. Build the options table: at least one non-training option, one mixed option and the course as asked. For each, what it fixes, what it does not, and what it needs from the sponsor (a decision, access, a process change, time from managers). Keeping the course on the table is deliberate: the sponsor chooses, they are not cornered.
5. Propose the criteria to agree before choosing: the measure that should move, the date, and the evidence both sides will accept. Agreeing these first turns "your opinion against mine" into a shared test.
6. List the likely objections with a reply grounded in the evidence. No debating tricks; where the objection is fair, the reply says so and adjusts the option. Then leave the agreement record blank for you to fill after the meeting: what was agreed, who does what, by when, and what happens if the measure does not move.

## Output Format
```markdown
# Sponsor Conversation One-Pager
## The problem we share
[Role] are [actual] and need to be [desired], shown by [measure]. Evidence: [the two or three findings, each with its source]
## What was asked, and what it is for
| Position (what was asked) | Likely interest (to confirm in the meeting) | Question to ask |
|---|---|---|
| [a course by [date]] | [number, deadline, risk or visibility] | [open question] |
## Options
| Option | What it fixes | What it does not fix | What it needs from the sponsor |
|---|---|---|---|
| [non-training fix] | [cause] | [cause] | [decision, access, process change, managers' time] |
| [mixed: fix plus short training] | [cause] | [cause] | [need] |
| [the course as asked] | [cause] | [cause] | [need] |
## Criteria to agree first
Measure that should move: [measure] | By: [date] | Evidence both sides accept: [source]
## Likely objections
| Objection | Reply from the evidence | What we could adjust |
|---|---|---|
| [objection] | [finding and source] | [change to an option] |
## Agreement record (fill after the meeting)
Agreed: [ ] | Who does what: [ ] | By when: [ ] | If the measure does not move: [ ]
## Decision
[Sponsor] and [L&D lead] choose the option and confirm the criteria by [date].
```

## Done When
- The page opens with the problem line and evidence, and fits on one page.
- Every interest is marked "to confirm in the meeting" and has a question beside it.
- The options table holds a non-training option, a mixed option and the course as asked, each with what it needs from the sponsor.
- The criteria line names a measure, a date and a source, and the agreement record is blank and ready to fill.

## Quality Bar
- Every reply to an objection points to a finding in the diagnosis; nothing new is claimed for the meeting.
- Interests are hypotheses about what the role answers for, never a profile of the sponsor as a person, and there is no persuasion script.
- If the decision was already taken above the sponsor, the page says so and narrows to what can still change.
- Claude prepares the page; you hold the conversation, and the sponsor and the L&D lead decide what gets built.
