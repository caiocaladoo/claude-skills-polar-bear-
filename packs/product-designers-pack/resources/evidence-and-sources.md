# Evidence and sources

Research checked 11 October 2026.

## What this pack claims

The pack claims that each skill applies a named method or a documented practice faithfully, attributes it to its originator through a public source, and states where it breaks. It claims that the order of the pack and the "When to run it" lines come from what product designers say they struggle with.

Nothing in the pack is legal advice: accessibility duties, consent, personal data and consumer protection points are framed as questions for a qualified adviser.

## Deliberate design choices

These are 90n's own design decisions, not findings from the sources.

- **The red line.** Claude drafts, checks and explains; you make the design decisions, real users test the work, and nothing is presented as tested, accessible or ready to ship that was not. The sources support the parts (tools cannot check all of accessibility, a heuristic pass cannot replace users, a prototype is for ideas and buy-in), and the single line is ours.
- **A person's check before anything is shown.** The share check before a prototype leaves the team, the "cannot tell from a design" answer in the accessibility review, and Claude's critique pass labelled as a second opinion are our own design.
- **Chosen or inherited.** The sameness check's mechanics (list the default choices, mark each as chosen on purpose or inherited) are ours, anchored on one article about why generated output converges. It lists and asks; it never rates taste.
- **Present, missing or unclear.** The ready check inside the handoff spec allows three answers only, so it checks completeness and never drifts into judging the design. A ready checklist is a common team convention, described here with no single originator.
- **States as a table.** The list of interface states (default, loading, empty, error, partial, success and others) is common practice, described generically.
- **Contribution, not attribution.** The impact write-up says what design contributed and names what else moved. This is our standard, not a rule from the HEART paper.
- **No scoring of people.** Severity, value and effort describe problems in the work. Nothing rates, ranks or profiles a designer, a participant or a colleague, and comments are logged by role.
- **Adviser referrals.** Accessibility duties, consent, personal data and other legal points end with "check with a qualified adviser". The skills do not say what any law requires.
- **Roles only.** The pack names the reader's role and nothing else: no company size, sector or type, so each skill starts from the work in front of you.
- **Tools named plainly.** A Claude surface or a connector is named only where a public Anthropic page describes what it does, and every one is optional.
