# Offer Negotiation Prep

## When To Use
The offer is good, you know you should ask, and you have nothing but a feeling. You have years of trade-off conversations behind you and have rarely run one about your own terms. This answers the question: what exactly will I ask for, on what basis, and what will I do if the answer is no?

## When Not To Use
Not for choosing between options: Offer Comparison does that. Not for the first mention of pay before an offer exists: Recruiter Screen Prep covers that call. And it is preparation only. Claude is not part of the conversation itself.

## Inputs
- The offer in writing, and the range the employer stated for the role, if they gave one
- Your current terms, any other written offer, and your Offer Comparison if you ran one
- Any pay data you bring yourself, with its source and the date you read it
If you have none of this, I start from the offer as you describe it and your alternative in your own words, and mark the output as a first draft.

## Approach
BATNA, the best alternative to a negotiated agreement, as set out by the Program on Negotiation at Harvard Law School (What is BATNA). Its rules shape the prep: the alternative must be realistic, compared on equivalent terms, translated to the deal on the table before you give anything up, and reassessed as facts change. The failure this prevents is negotiating against an alternative you hope for, such as an offer that has not arrived, and finding out mid-call that you had less room than you spoke as if you had.

## Workflow
1. I ask up to three questions: what you will really do if this does not close (stay, take another live offer, keep searching); which items matter most to you; and what the employer has said so far about range, level and timing.
2. The alternative first. I write it as it is, on the same terms as the offer, and note what would have to change for it to get better or worse. A hoped-for offer is written as hoped-for, not as an alternative.
3. Per item (pay, level, scope, start date, flexibility, anything else you name): what you want, the least you would accept, and the fact behind the ask with its source and date. Sources allowed: the employer's stated range, another written offer, your current terms, data you bring. An ask with no fact is labelled a preference, which is honest and allowed.
4. Their likely constraints, marked as guesses: pay bands, parity inside the team, budget cycles, the start date they need. Each one becomes a question for you to ask, not a position to argue against.
5. The order of the conversation: thanks and real interest; your questions; the most important ask with its reason; what you can flex on; what happens next and by when. Ask about the whole package before settling any single item.
6. The lines you will say, short and in your words. A stated alternative must be real, and nothing is bluffed. Voice mode lets you say the lines aloud and hear how they sound, for rehearsal only: the real call is yours. Contract and legal points (notice, restrictive clauses, equity documents) are listed as written for a qualified adviser.

## Output Format
```markdown
# Offer Negotiation Prep
## Your alternative if this does not close
[What you will really do, on the same terms as the offer. What would change it: [fact]. Reviewed on [date].]
## Asks
| Item | What you want | Least you would accept | Fact behind the ask | Source and date | Fact or preference |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| [pay, level, scope, start date, flexibility] | [your ask] | [your floor] | [fact, or none] | [stated range, written offer, current terms, your data, date] | [fact or preference] |
## Their likely constraints (guesses)
| Possible constraint | Question you will ask |
|---|---|
| [band, parity, budget cycle, start date need] | [question] |
## Order of the conversation
| Step | Your line |
|---|---|
| [thanks and interest; questions first; most important ask and its reason; where you can flex; what happens next and by when] | [your words] |
## Terms for a qualified adviser
| Term | Wording as written |
|---|---|
| [notice, restrictive clause, equity document] | [quoted text] |
## Decision
You decide [what to ask for, and what you will accept] by [date], and you hold the conversation with [recruiter or hiring manager, by role] on [date].
```

## Done When
- The alternative is one you could act on this week, written on the same terms as the offer
- Every ask has a want, a floor, and either a sourced fact or the label "preference"
- Every guessed constraint has a question beside it, and legal points sit in their own table
- The lines are short enough to say aloud and sound like you

## Quality Bar
- No market rate, benchmark or "typical" uplift comes from Claude. A missing figure stays a [placeholder].
- No line pressures or misleads the person across the table, and nothing is written about them as people.
- Contract and legal points are listed, not explained: check with a qualified adviser.
- Every ask rests on a fact you can source or is named as a preference; no market figure comes from Claude, nothing is bluffed, and you hold the conversation yourself.
