# Evidence and sources

Research checked 11 October 2026.

## What this pack claims

**It claims:** each skill applies a named method or produces a standard artifact of instructional design; each named method is attributed through a public page, an institution guide, a standard or a peer-reviewed paper; and the research below supports the design choices the skills make (less extraneous content on screen, practice with feedback, retrieval and spacing, checks planned before content, a person checking what an AI drafted).

**What it says about Claude:** only what Anthropic's own pages said on the research date (sources C1 to C6). Documents, slide decks, visual prototypes and small interactive tools can be made in a conversation and exported in the formats those pages list. No Anthropic page read for this pack describes an export to a standard e-learning package, reporting of completion or results to an LMS, or embedding of current artifacts in a course page, so no skill promises any of them. A shared artifact needs a Claude account to open. Anthropic's pages differ on which surfaces are still in beta and on which plans, so check your own plan.

## Deliberate design choices

These are Polar Bear's choices, not findings from the sources. The red line is ours: Claude drafts and checks, but you decide what the learner needs, you run through the course yourself before anyone else does, and a named person signs it off; nothing invented is passed off as real, and no learner's result is ever used to grade them. So are the rules that follow from it. The check group is the centre of the pack, and each check is run by a person and ends in a decision a named person makes. On facts, Claude flags and never fixes: the expert corrects. The designer's own run-through is a rule, and Claude prepares the protocol and sorts the notes without taking the course. The usability methods (the walkthrough and thinking aloud) were written for interfaces and are adapted here to courses; pilot notes are about the course, never about the person trying it. Item analysis is used at question level and in aggregate only, small groups are left out, and no skill produces a result about a named learner, reviewer or expert. Estimates use the designer's own past builds, because published development-time ratios disagree. The interactive practice skill is framed as a prototype and a pilot, because a shared artifact needs a Claude account and nothing is tracked. Accessibility-law, copyright and other legal points are marked "check with a qualified adviser". The pack names the role it is for and nothing else about the reader, and it names no authoring tool, LMS or other vendor.
