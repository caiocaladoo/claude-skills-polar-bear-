# Action Mapping Workshop

## When To Use
The sponsor's list is everything people should "know" and nothing they should do. Use this when a course is agreed and you can get the sponsor and one expert together for an hour. It answers: what must people do on the job for the goal to move, and how do you get the room to agree that before the content list hardens?

## When Not To Use
If nobody has agreed that anything should be built, run Course Brief first; this session assumes a yes. If the actions are already signed and you need conditions and criteria, go to Measurable Learning Objectives. For content with no business measure at all, a map has nothing to hang from; say so to the sponsor.

## Inputs
- The agreed brief or the request, the role in scope, the sponsor's list of what people should know, and who will be in the room, by role. After the session: your raw notes, the board typed up, or the transcript, pasted in.
If you have none of this, I start from the sponsor's list and mark the output as a first draft, with a blank map for you to fill in the room.

## Approach
Action mapping, from its originator's public page (blog.cathy-moore.com, "Action mapping: a visual approach to training design"): one measurable business goal, what people must do on the job to reach it, why they are not doing it, then practice for each action and only the information that practice needs. Run as a live session, the method does its real work: the sponsor hears the expert say what people get wrong. The failure it prevents: you fill the map alone, email it, and the sponsor replies with the original list pasted underneath and "please also include". You facilitate; Claude prepares the sheet beforehand and tidies your notes afterwards.

## Workflow
1. Ask at most three questions: what measure does the sponsor already watch, who is the one expert who does or supervises the task, and how many minutes do you have for each of the five blocks?
2. Draft the pre-read, half a page: the goal format `[measure] will [change] by [date] as [role] [do what]`, the request to bring one recent case, and one line saying the list of topics will be sorted in the room, not defended beforehand. Then build the run of show in five blocks with your timings: goal, actions, why not, practice, information. If no measure exists, the map says "no measure agreed" and the first block's only job is to find one. Each block gets an opening line you can say aloud.
3. Actions block: a brain-dump of what people must do, in verbs you could watch, then the room picks the few that matter most for the goal. You hold the line on "understand" and "be aware"; ask what a person who understands does differently. Then the why-not pass, one action at a time: skill, knowledge, tool, motivation or environment. Causes describe the work, never who is weak. Actions blocked by a tool or the environment go to the sponsor as a non-training fix.
4. Practice, then information: for each remaining action, one realistic decision the learner would make, then only what that decision needs. For every "they need to know X", ask "what do they do with X on the job?" An action goes on the map; anything else goes to the parking list with a place to live.
5. Read the cut list back before the hour ends, each item with its reason and parking place. Afterwards, paste your notes and Claude tidies them into the map for sign-off, marking anything it could not read as `[to confirm with the expert]`.

## Done When
- There is one goal with the sponsor's own measure, or the plain line "no measure agreed".
- Every action on the map is a verb someone could watch, with a cause and either a practice idea or another fix with an owner.
- Every item from the sponsor's list is on the map or on the cut list, the cut list was read back in the room, and the map marks what still needs the expert.

## Quality Bar
- The measure, the target and the date are the sponsor's, and the timings are yours; the plan proposes the order, not the numbers.
- The map stops at actions and practice ideas; conditions, criteria and wording come later.
- The goal measures a business result, never a named person or team.
- Claude prepares the session and tidies the map; you run the room, and the sponsor and the expert sign the goal and the actions.
