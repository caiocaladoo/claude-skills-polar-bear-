# Evidence and sources

Research checked 11 October 2026.

## What these skills claim

They claim that each skill applies a public method or a common practice standard faithfully, attributes a named method to its originator through a public source, and says where the method breaks. They claim that the order of the seven groups follows what public reviews of training research describe: what happens before and after a session matters alongside the session itself. They claim that the "When to run it" lines come from what trainers and facilitators say they struggle with.

The same goes for the well-known group methods described without a source in the activity bank and the openers (silent writing followed by a round robin and a vote, think then pair then share, a check-in round, dot voting).

## Deliberate design choices

These are 90n's choices, not findings from the sources above. The red line is ours: Claude prepares and a person runs the room; nothing in these skills acts during a live session. No skill grades, ranks, scores or profiles a participant, a trainer or a manager, and no skill sorts people into personality or style types; feedback rates the session, observer notes go only to the learner, barrier logs record conditions and never people, and results are read in aggregate with small groups suppressed. Consent wording is written as a page participants can read and refuse, and capture happens from the wall in a break, with transcripts only where participants agreed to recording. Recording, privacy and data questions differ by place and contract, so those points end with "check with a qualified adviser". Bringing the sponsor and the managers in before the build, tying every opener to the first content block, and offering a way to take part without personal disclosure are our reading of the struggles, not a tested rule. Every duration, gap, share and threshold is left for the trainer to set, the proposal leaves the price blank, and the skills name the role only, never a kind of organisation. No vendor's tool is named: the skills say "your video platform", "your board" and "your survey tool".
