# Training Session Plan

## When To Use
The draft is a list of topics with a slide count next to each, and practice is "if we have time". Use this when people must leave the room able to do something, and you are the one delivering. It answers: what happens in each block, and where exactly do people do the thing?

## When Not To Use
If the group must leave having produced or decided something, that is a Workshop Agenda; a mixed day uses both, one per half. If the content does not fit the slot, run Session Time Budget first and bring its result here.

## Inputs
- What people should be able to do at work afterwards, who they are by role, how many, and the slot.
- The content or slides you have, real problems from the job (a case, a message, a mistake), what the group told you beforehand, and the share of time you want for practice.
If you have none of this, I start from the topic, the role and the slot and mark the output as a first draft.

## Approach
Gagné's nine events of instruction (Northern Illinois University's teaching centre page), grouped into five blocks, and opened the way Merrill's First Principles of Instruction (2002) point: learning is promoted when learners work on real problems. The judgment is that you set the practice share before anything else, because showing always overruns and practice is what it eats. The failure this prevents: a session that goes almost entirely to slides, leaves "any questions?" for the end, and gets silence.

## Workflow
1. Ask at most three questions: what should people be able to do at work afterwards, how long is the slot and what share of it do you give to practice, and which real problem from the job can we open on?
2. Fix `[share of time for practice]` first and reserve those minutes. Showing is fitted into what is left; if showing overruns, content is cut, never practice.
3. Map the nine events into five blocks. Open: gain attention, inform of objectives. Recall: stimulate recall of prior learning. Show: present the content, provide learning guidance. Practise: elicit performance, provide feedback, assess performance. Apply: enhance retention and transfer.
4. Script the first five minutes: a real problem from the job that people react to before any agenda slide, then the objectives in one line, as what they will be able to do. A session that opens on housekeeping teaches the room to wait.
5. Write the energy logic: one line per part of the day saying why this block sits here, with hard practice where energy is high and paired or moving work in the dip. What you know about this group overrides it.
6. Make "assess performance" feedback in the room, from you or from peers, against what good looks like. Nothing is recorded against a person.
7. Reserve the last block for the If-Then Action Plan, with its minutes shown, then check that the timings add up to the slot.

## Output Format
```markdown
# Training Session Plan
Session: [title] | Group: [role, number] | Slot: [start to end] | Practice share: [share of time for practice, set by the trainer]
After this session people can: [one line per objective]
## First five minutes
Problem shown: [the case, message or mistake] | Question to the room: [question] | How people answer: [pairs / a written line / hands] | Objectives, said afterwards: [one line]
## Timed flow
| Time | Block | Events it serves | What people do | What I do | Materials |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| [time] | Open | Attention, objectives | [react to the problem] | [action] | [materials] |
| [time] | Recall | Prior learning | [activity] | [action] | [materials] |
| [time] | Show | Content, guidance | [activity] | [action] | [materials] |
| [time] | Practise | Performance, feedback, assessment in the room | [the task itself] | [feedback given] | [materials] |
| [time] | Apply | Retention and transfer | [fill in the If-Then Action Plan] | [action] | [the page] |
## Energy logic
| Part of the day | Block placed here | Why here |
|---|---|---|
| [part] | [block] | [reason] |
## Checks
Minutes: show [minutes] | practise [minutes] | the rest [minutes] | total [minutes] of [slot] | Cut if showing overruns: [content item], which goes to [handout / follow-up]
## Decision
[Trainer] confirms the practice share and the flow by [date], and tells [sponsor] what was cut and where it went.
```

## Done When
- Every objective has a practise block where people do it, not hear about it.
- The first five minutes open on a real problem from the job, and the objectives come after it.
- The practice minutes match the share you set, and the timings add up to the slot.
- The last block is reserved for the action plan, with its minutes shown.

## Quality Bar
- Practice is protected: content is cut before practice is.
- Every duration and share is a placeholder you set; no norm for attention, block length or group size.
- No block is marked diverge or converge, and no group split is designed for a decision; that belongs to the agenda.
- Claude drafts the flow; you run the room and change it, and performance in practice is feedback in the moment, never a grade kept on a person.
