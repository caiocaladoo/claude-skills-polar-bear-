# If-Then Action Plan

## When To Use
The close is a round of "one takeaway each", and by Monday nobody remembers theirs. Use this while you design the last block, so each participant leaves with one plan written in their own hand and tied to a moment they will meet at work. It answers one question: what will this person do, and what will tell them it is time to do it?

## When Not To Use
If you want the questions you ask aloud to close an activity, run Activity Debrief Questions; this is the written page that follows them. If you want one task you offer to the whole group, run Practice Assignment. And if the last block has no time to write in, win the time back with Session Time Budget first: a plan filled in while people pack their bags is a takeaway round on paper.

## Inputs
- The session's learning points, or the plan or slide outline they can be read from.
- Who is in the room, by role, and the situations at work where the skill should show up.
- How long the last block is and whether people write on paper or on a screen.
If you have none of this, I start from the session title and one thing people practised, and mark the output as a first draft.

## Approach
The method is implementation intentions, as reviewed in Gollwitzer and Sheeran's 2006 meta-analysis of implementation intentions: if-then plans help people act on goals they already hold. That last clause is the whole design. A plan the trainer wrote, or a plan handed in at the door, stops being the person's own goal and becomes a form. The other failure this prevents is the list: five good intentions, each tied to nothing, all gone by the first full inbox.

## Workflow
1. Ask at most three questions: which learning points could a participant choose from, which real situations at work call for them, and how many minutes does the last block give for writing?
2. Build the blank page around ONE thing. One box, not five: a participant who wants to write three is asked to pick the one they would be sorry to lose.
3. Fix the sentence: "If [a specific situation I will meet at work], then I will [one specific action]." The situation is a cue they can recognise, such as a meeting, a message or a moment in a conversation. A date or a mood is not a cue, and "I will try to listen more" is not an action.
4. Add the first date the situation could happen, in their own calendar terms, then one obstacle with its own second sentence: "If [obstacle], then I will [what I do instead]."
5. Add "who I will tell" with three equal options: a peer, their manager, nobody. It is their choice, and the page says so.
6. Write the trainer's two-minute script: why one plan, how to pick a cue, that the page is theirs and nobody collects it. Then write two worked examples from the session's own learning points, marked as examples, so nobody copies them as the right answer.
7. Hand over a check for you to run: fill in the page yourself as a participant would, in the time the block allows. If you cannot finish, or your "if" is a date, the page or the block changes.

## Output Format
```markdown
# If-Then Action Plan Page
Session: [title] | Time to fill it in: [minutes] | This page is yours. Nobody collects it.
## My one thing
The one thing I will try: [in my own words]
## My plan
If [a specific situation I will meet at work], then I will [one specific action].
The first time this could happen: [date or occasion in my calendar]
## What could get in the way
If [obstacle], then I will [what I do instead].
## Who I will tell
[A peer / my manager / nobody]: [name, if any, and when]
## Trainer script (two minutes, not on the participant page)
[Why one plan] [How to pick a cue] [The page is yours and is not collected]
## Worked examples (examples only, built from this session)
| Learning point | If (the cue) | Then (the action) | Obstacle plan |
|---|---|---|---|
| [learning point] | [situation] | [action] | [if obstacle, then] |
## Decision
[Trainer] decides whether the page goes into the last block as written and how many minutes it gets, by [date before the session].
```

## Done When
- The page asks for one plan and has room for only one.
- Every "if" on the examples is a situation someone could recognise as it happens, and every "then" is one action.
- The two examples come from this session's learning points and are marked as examples.
- You have filled the page in yourself within the time the block allows.

## Quality Bar
- The script fits in two minutes and says aloud that the page is not collected.
- No field asks for a rating, a level or a self-assessment.
- "Nobody" is a full option for who they will tell, not a fallback.
- The source is described as it is: it concerns goals a person already holds, and the page promises no result.
- The plan belongs to the participant: you never write it for them, collect it or report on it.
