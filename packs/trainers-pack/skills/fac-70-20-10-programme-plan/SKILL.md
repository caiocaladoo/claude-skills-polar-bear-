# 70-20-10 Programme Plan
## When To Use
The client wants the two-day course again, and you know two days will not do it. You have a session that works in the room and a skill that only forms at work. This answers: what will people do, with whom, in their real job, so that the course becomes one part of the learning and not all of it?

## When Not To Use
If you need one task after one session, run Practice Assignment: this plan designs several assignments across weeks and needs a client who can supply real work. If managers have agreed to take part and you need their conversations, run Manager Check-In Guide. If the client cannot offer real assignments at all, say so and keep the session.

## Inputs
- The session that exists (its plan or outline), the skill it teaches, and the real work coming up for the participants' role (projects, recurring tasks, problems that land on the team).
- Who could coach or pair up, by role, and what the client has agreed to put in.
If you have none of this, I start from the session title and one capability and mark the output as a first draft.

## Approach
70-20-10, from the Center for Creative Leadership, names three sources of development: challenging assignments, developmental relationships, and coursework and training. The Center calls it a classic guideline. This skill adds its own rule: no percentage targets, because the judgement is in choosing the assignment that teaches this specific thing, not in filling a ratio. The failure it prevents: a programme slide that says most of the learning happens on the job with nothing behind it, so "on the job" means "work it out alone" and the course is still the only thing anyone can point to.

## Workflow
1. Ask at most three questions: which two or three capabilities from the session matter most at work, what real work in the next [period] could stretch people on them, and what has the client agreed to provide (managers' time, assignments, people to coach)?
2. Start from the session that exists. Name each capability in one line, in words that describe something a person does. A capability you cannot tie to real work is parked, not planned.
3. Assignments: choose real work that forces each capability, such as a first client call, leading a handover or fixing a live process. "Shadow for a week" teaches watching. Give each an owner by role, a support person and a check-in date. Assignments are offered, and a person may decline.
4. Relationships: coaching and peer pairs tied to an assignment, never floating. Say who meets whom by role, how often, and the first topic. "Find a mentor" is not a plan.
5. Live sessions: keep only what the assignments need people to know or practise first, and place each one just before the assignment that uses it. Cut the existing course to fit the work, not the other way round, and list what was cut and why.
6. Check against the work, not a ratio: every capability has at least one assignment and one relationship. If a capability truly needs more time in the room, the plan says so plainly. Then list what the client must put in place for the plan to run, each with a role and a date. A plan that depends on assignments nobody has agreed to release is a course with decoration.

## Output Format
```markdown
# 70-20-10 Programme Plan
Built from: [existing session] | For: [participants' role] | Period: [start to end] | Owner: [role]
## Capabilities
1. [capability, as something a person does] 2. [capability] 3. [capability]
## Assignments at work
| Assignment | Capability it forces | Owner | Support person | Check-in date |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| [real task or project] | [capability] | [role] | [role] | [date] |
## Coaching and peer pairs
| Tied to assignment | Who meets whom | How often | First topic |
|---|---|---|---|
| [assignment] | [role and role] | [cadence] | [topic] |
## Live sessions
| Session | What it prepares | Placed before | Length |
|---|---|---|---|
| [session] | [knowledge or practice] | [assignment] | [minutes] |

Cut from the existing course: [content] because [reason]
## What the client puts in place
| Condition | From whom | By when |
|---|---|---|
| [managers' time / assignments available / people to coach] | [role] | [date] |
## Decision
[Sponsor name] confirms the assignments and the support people by [date]. [Trainer name] confirms which sessions stay and where they sit by [date].
```

## Done When
- Every capability has at least one assignment and one relationship.
- Every assignment has an owner by role, a support person and a check-in date.
- Every live session sits just before the assignment that uses it, and the cut list has reasons.
- No percentage appears anywhere in the plan.

## Quality Bar
- Assignments are work the role already has to do, never "exposure" or shadowing alone.
- Each pair is tied to a named assignment and has a first topic.
- The session list is shorter than the original course, or the plan says why not.
- Lengths, periods and cadences are placeholders you set. Check-in dates are for support and feed no appraisal.
- Assignments are offered and supported; nothing in the plan records or ranks who did well.
