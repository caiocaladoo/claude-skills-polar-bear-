# Train-the-Trainer Handover

## When To Use
The programme has to run in five places and the plan is one walkthrough of the deck. You built the session, you know where it bites, and colleagues will soon stand in front of a room with it. This answers: how does each of them practise the hard parts before real participants see them, and how do you agree together how their first delivery runs?

## When Not To Use
If there is no written guide to the session yet, write it first with Trainer and Facilitator Guide: this handover is the practice that makes that document usable, and it does not replace it. If you want the same method for learners and not for trainers, run Role-Play Practice Kit.

## Inputs
- The guide and the plan for each module, and your Difficult Moments Plan if you have one.
- Who is taking the session on (role and delivery experience), the first delivery date, and the moments in your own deliveries where it nearly went wrong.
If you have none of this, I start from the module list and the trainers' roles and mark the output as a first draft.

## Approach
Behaviour modelling applied to trainers, following the 2005 meta-analysis of behaviour modelling training by Taylor, Russ-Eft and Chan: clear learning points, a model to watch, practice, then feedback against the same points. Here the points are about delivery, not content, and the model is you. The failure this prevents: a "certification" day with a scorecard, where the nervous trainers hide their weakest module and meet it for the first time in front of a room.

## Workflow
1. Ask at most three questions: how many trainers are taking this on and how much have they delivered, which module is hardest to run, and can you or a co-trainer be in the room for first deliveries?
2. Write four to six delivery learning points: short rules for running THIS session, such as "hand the question back to the room before answering". If a point would fit any session, it is too general; rewrite it from a moment that went wrong for you.
3. Plan the model. You run one segment live against those points, and the group names where each point showed. Choose a segment with a real turn in it, not the easy opening.
4. Plan the teach-back. Each trainer delivers a segment of [minutes], then hears from peers against the same points: what worked, and one thing to try. Nobody rates anybody.
5. Build practice rounds that add difficulty one situation at a time, drawn from the Difficult Moments Plan: a clean run, then a silent room, then one voice filling the room, then a question they cannot answer. Situations only, never a kind of participant.
6. Write delivery notes per module: what trips trainers up, what to stress, where timing slips. These come from your own deliveries, so I mark every note I had to guess for you to confirm.
7. Write the readiness conversation, with no score: the trainer's own view, the support they want, and a first-delivery arrangement agreed together (alone, with a co-trainer, or after another round).

## Output Format
```markdown
# Train-the-Trainer Handover Kit
Session: [name] | Handed over by: [original trainer] | Trainers: [number, role] | First delivery: [date]
## Delivery learning points
1. [short rule for running this session] 2. [short rule] 3. [short rule]
## The modelled segment and teach-back slots
| Slot | Segment | Run by | Time | Feedback against |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Model | [segment] | [original trainer] | [minutes] | [the group names where each point showed] |
| [teach-back slot] | [segment] | [trainer role] | [minutes] | [learning points, from peers] |
## Practice rounds
| Round | Situation added | What to practise |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | None | [clean run] |
| [2 to 4] | [silent room / one voice filling the room / a question you cannot answer] | [prepared move] |
## Delivery notes per module
| Module | What trips trainers up | What to stress | Where timing slips |
|---|---|---|---|
| [module] | [note] | [note] | [note] |
## Readiness conversation (no score)
Your view of delivering [module]: [their words] | Support you want: [support]
First delivery, agreed together: [alone / with a co-trainer / after another round]
## Decision
[Original trainer] and each trainer agree the first-delivery arrangement by [date]. [Programme owner] confirms co-trainer cover for those dates by [date].
```

## Done When
- Every trainer delivers at least one segment and hears feedback against the learning points.
- Each practice round adds exactly one situation, and every module has delivery notes you have confirmed.
- The readiness section has no score, rating, pass mark or checklist total, and your part in first deliveries has dates.

## Quality Bar
- Learning points are about delivery in this session; content accuracy lives in the guide.
- Feedback describes what happened in the segment against a point, never the trainer as a person.
- Practice situations are described as conditions in the room, never as participant types.
- Every time in the kit is a placeholder you set, and notes from the practice stay with the trainer concerned, never with a manager.
- Readiness is a conversation, never a score, a certificate ranking or a comparison between trainers.
