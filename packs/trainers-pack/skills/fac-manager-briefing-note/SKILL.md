# Manager Briefing Note

## When To Use
The manager approved the day off and has no idea what it was for. Use this for any session, even when you have no access to the organisation beyond the sponsor, to tell managers in under a minute what their person is doing and the one small thing that is theirs to do. It answers one question: what does a busy manager need to know, ask and make room for?

## When Not To Use
If managers have agreed to take a real part, with conversations and check-ins, run Manager Check-In Guide; this note is two messages and nothing else. Messages to participants belong in Post-Session Follow-Up Sequence. If the participants have no manager in the picture (an open-enrolment group, a room of peers from different places), skip it.

## Inputs
- What the session covers and what people will practise, from the plan or the purpose brief.
- The one thing people are asked to do afterwards (the practice task and its window, if there is one).
- Who can reach the managers: you, or the sponsor on your behalf.
If you have none of this, I start from the session title, its date and one thing people will practise, and mark the output as a first draft.

## Approach
Grossman and Salas's 2011 review of training transfer, and Burke and Hutchins's 2007 review before it, place support and follow-up in the work environment among the things linked to whether training gets used. A note cannot create that support; it can remove the excuse of not knowing. The failure it prevents is the well-meant manager who, having heard nothing, fills their person's first week back with everything that piled up, and the other kind who asks the trainer "so how did mine do?" because nobody told them that question has no answer here.

## Workflow
1. Ask at most three questions: what is the one thing you want managers to make room for afterwards, who sends the note (you or the sponsor), and how do managers in this organisation usually get short messages?
2. Write the BEFORE message. What the session covers, in three lines and in the manager's vocabulary, not the course catalogue's. The one question to ask their person beforehand: "what do you want to get from it?" The one thing to make room for afterwards, with its window.
3. Write the AFTER message. What was covered, in three lines. The one question to ask: "what did you try, and what got in the way?" The one thing to make room for now.
4. End both with what the manager will NOT receive: no attendance judgement, no report, no feedback on any individual. If they want to know how it went, they ask their person.
5. Cut each message until it reads in under a minute. One question and one ask each; a second ask halves the chance of the first.
6. Add the sender line and, where the sponsor sends on your behalf, a one-line cover for the sponsor. Then stop: no conversation guide, no check-in dates, no log. Those belong to managers who agreed to more.

## Output Format
```markdown
# Manager Briefing Note
Session: [title] | Date: [date] | Sender: [trainer / sponsor on the trainer's behalf] | Channel: [how managers get short messages]
## Message 1: before the session (send [days] before)
Subject: [subject line]
What it covers: [line one] / [line two] / [line three]
One question to ask your person: "What do you want to get from it?"
One thing to make room for afterwards: [the practice task] within [window]
What you will not receive: no attendance judgement, no report and no feedback on any individual. To hear how it went, ask your person.
## Message 2: after the session (send [days] after)
Subject: [subject line]
What was covered: [line one] / [line two] / [line three]
One question to ask your person: "What did you try, and what got in the way?"
One thing to make room for now: [the task or the occasion]
What you will not receive: no attendance judgement, no report and no feedback on any individual. To hear how it went, ask your person.
## Cover line for the sponsor (if the sponsor sends)
[One line]
## Decision
[Trainer] approves the two messages and [sponsor or sender] decides whether and when they go to managers, by [date before the session].
```

## Done When
- There are exactly two messages, and each reads in under a minute.
- Each has three lines on content, one question and one thing to make room for.
- Both end with what the manager will not receive.
- The sender is named, and the sponsor has a cover line if the sponsor sends.

## Quality Bar
- Written in words a manager uses about the work, with no training jargon.
- The ask is something a manager can do inside a normal week.
- No promise of a result, and no request that the manager check, observe or assess anyone.
- Nothing in the note names or describes a participant.
- Managers get what the session covered and one question to ask; they never get a report on any individual.
