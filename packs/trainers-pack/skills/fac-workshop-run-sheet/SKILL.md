# Workshop Run Sheet

## When To Use
The session is Thursday, the agenda is agreed, and nothing is runnable yet. Use this when the design is settled and the team running it this week needs to know what happens at which clock time, who holds it, and what they do when it slips. It works for a training session as well as a decision workshop.

## When Not To Use
If the blocks, exercises or timings are still open, that is design and Workshop Agenda fits. If someone else will deliver this session later without you, write a Trainer and Facilitator Guide. For a full situation-by-situation plan, use Difficult Moments Plan; this sheet carries one line per row.

## Inputs
- The agreed agenda or session plan, with block lengths and the exercise in each block.
- The facilitation team and what each person does, the start time and the hard stop, room or platform, and headcount.
- The "come with" ask from the invitation, so the block that uses it can be protected.
If you have none of this, I start from the start time, the end time and the list of blocks, and mark the output as a first draft.

## Approach
This is a practice standard among facilitation teams with no single originator: an agenda says what happens, a run sheet says what each person does, segment by segment, including when things wobble. The judgment is in what gets scripted. Pivots are written out, because that is where a session loses minutes; the facilitation itself never is, because a facilitator reading a script stops reading the room. The failure it prevents: adding up durations in your head mid-afternoon, then cutting the close to catch up.

## Workflow
1. Ask at most three questions: who is on the team and who runs, watches time and captures; is it a room or a platform and for how many people; and what are the start time and the hard stop?
2. Split the agenda into segments finer than its blocks: arrival, opening, each exercise's setup, work and harvest, transitions, breaks, close. Give each a clock time, not a duration. If the segments do not fit, I list the problem under "Sent back to the agenda" and do not redesign silently.
3. Fill the Who column for every row: one person runs it, one watches time, one captures. A row with no owner is not done.
4. Write "Say this" for the pivots only: the two or three sentences that set up a block or land its point. The middle of each block stays yours.
5. Write one wobble move per row, about the session and never about a person: the fallback if it lands flat, and which block shrinks if you are behind. The fuller plan lives in the Difficult Moments Plan.
6. Flag the two protected moments: the close, and the block where the "come with" preparation is used. Preparation that is never used teaches people to skip it.
7. Count the kit against the exercises and the headcount, for a room or for a platform, then write the day-before and morning-of checklists at ten lines each at most. If the sheet passes three pages, I merge segments.

## Output Format
```markdown
# Workshop Run Sheet
Session: [title] | Date: [date] | Room or platform: [place] | Headcount: [number] | Team: [names and roles]
## Run sheet
| Time | What happens | Who (runs / time / capture) | Say this | Materials | If it wobbles |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| [clock time] | [segment in one line] | [name / name / name] | [two or three sentences for the pivot] | [worksheet, wall space or board link] | [the move; which block shrinks if behind] |
## Protected moments
| Moment | Clock time | What gives way instead |
|---|---|---|
| The close | [clock time] | [block that shrinks] |
| The block that uses the "come with" preparation | [clock time] | [block that shrinks] |
## Kit list
| Item | Needed for | Count for [headcount] | Who brings or tests it |
|---|---|---|---|
| [item, with paper size where printed] | [exercise] | [count] | [name] |
## Checklists (ten lines each at most)
- [ ] Day before: [check]
- [ ] Morning of: [check]
## Sent back to the agenda
- [design problem found while building, or "none"]
## Decision
[Lead facilitator] signs off the run sheet and kit list by [date]; [agenda owner] decides on anything sent back to the agenda by [date].
```

## Done When
- Every row has a clock time, an owner and a wobble move.
- The two protected moments are flagged, each with the block that gives way.
- The kit list is counted against the exercises and the headcount, both checklists are ten lines or fewer, and the sheet is three pages or fewer.

## Quality Bar
- Clock times and block lengths come from your agenda; I supply no timing norm and no fixed buffer.
- A co-facilitator who reads the sheet shortly before the session knows what to do at each time.
- The kit list covers what decides whether an exercise can happen. In a room: sticky notes, pens that write, dot stickers, printed worksheets, something to stick things up, screen and adapter, a visible timer, wall space, movable tables. On a platform: access tested for every participant, a warm-up for the tools, who writes in each group, a board with frames matching the blocks.
- Scripts cover the pivots only; you read the room and leave the sheet when it needs it, and no wobble move names a person.
