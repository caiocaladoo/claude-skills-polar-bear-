# Session Agreements and Consent

## When To Use
A recording tool joins the session by default and nobody was asked. Or the sponsor wants "the recording" and you have not told participants there will be one. Use this before the invitation goes out, to settle how the room works together, what stays in it, and what is captured, by whom, for whom.

## When Not To Use
Not for what you do when an agreement is tested in the room: that is Difficult Moments Plan. Not for the invitation itself: Pre-Work and Invitation only announces that this wording is coming. It is prepared before the session; in the room you present it and the group edits it.

## Inputs
- The session: purpose, who attends, in person or on your video platform, and who else will see what comes out of it.
- Any plan to record, transcribe or use AI note-taking: who asked for it, why, who would see it and how long it would be kept.
- Any rule the client or your contract already sets on confidentiality, recording or data.

If you have none of this, I start from the session type and the group's roles and mark the output as a first draft.

## Approach
Two anchors. The Chatham House Rule gives the confidentiality model: what was said may be used; who said it, and their affiliation, may not be revealed. It covers attribution only and says nothing about recording, so recording gets its own wording. The International Association of Facilitators' core competencies name creating and sustaining a participatory environment as part of the job, which is why the agreements are a proposal the room owns and not rules read at it. The failure this prevents: a note-taking tool joins the call unannounced, one person spots it halfway through, and the honest conversation you were hired for ends there.

## Workflow
1. Ask up to three questions: what this session needs people to be able to say out loud, whether anything will be recorded, transcribed or noted by AI and who asked for that, and who will see the outputs afterwards.
2. Draft a short list of working agreements in four areas: how we speak, how we disagree, phones and side work, how to pass. Keep each one a behaviour someone could see. Present it as a proposal with the line "what would you change or add?"
3. Write the confidentiality rule in plain words, modelled on the Rule: what is said here may be used; who said it, and where they work, is not passed on. Add what the sponsor will receive, so nobody has to guess.
4. If anything is captured, write the consent wording in five lines participants can read: what is captured, why, who will see it, how long it is kept, and how to say no without leaving the room. It is sent before the session and shown again at the start.
5. Write what happens if one person declines. The default is that the recording does not run. Name the alternatives: notes taken by a person, capture from the wall. Add your own note on default-on tools: check before the session whether any note-taking or recording feature joins automatically, and turn it off unless consent was given.
6. Mark every point that depends on law, contract or place. Recording, privacy and data rules differ: check with a qualified adviser.

## Output Format
```markdown
# Session Agreements and Consent Page
Session: [title] | Date: [date] | Prepared by: [trainer name]
## Working agreements (a proposal: what would you change or add?)
| Area | Proposed agreement | Edited by the room |
|---|---|---|
| How we speak | [agreement] | [their wording] |
| How we disagree | [agreement] | [their wording] |
| Phones and side work | [agreement] | [their wording] |
| How to pass | [agreement] | [their wording] |
## Confidentiality in plain words
[What is said here may be used. Who said it, and where they work, is not passed on.] The sponsor receives: [outputs, with no names attached].
## Recording, transcription and AI note-taking
| What is captured | Why | Who will see it | How long it is kept | How to say no without leaving |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| [nothing, or what] | [purpose] | [roles] | [period] | [how, and to whom] |
## If one person declines
Default: the recording does not run. Instead: [notes by a person, or capture from the wall].
## Checks (trainer, before the session)
- [ ] No note-taking or recording feature joins automatically, or it is turned off unless consent was given. Legal, privacy and data points to check with a qualified adviser: [list]
## Decision
[Trainer name] decides with [sponsor name] whether anything is recorded at all, by [date the invitation goes out]; the room edits and agrees the working agreements at the start.
```

## Done When
- The agreements fit on one screen and each one is a behaviour, not a value.
- The confidentiality rule says what the sponsor will and will not receive.
- The five consent lines are filled, or the page says plainly that nothing is recorded.
- The page says what happens when one person declines, and every legal, privacy or data point carries "check with a qualified adviser".

## Quality Bar
- The agreements are offered to the room, never imposed, and the room's edits replace the draft.
- Consent wording is readable by a participant in under a minute, with no legal phrasing.
- Saying no costs nothing: no leaving the room, no explaining why, no note of who declined.
- Nothing is recorded, transcribed or noted by AI without consent participants can read and refuse; one refusal is enough to turn it off.
