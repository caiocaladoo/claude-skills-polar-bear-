# Discovery Call Agenda

## When To Use
Your first calls are pleasant and shapeless. Forty minutes in you are still on background, the person who owns the problem never joined, and it ends with "let's stay in touch". Run this a day or two before any discovery conversation, to agree in writing what the call is for and how it may end.

## When Not To Use
This covers a single call. The steps from a yes to a start date belong in Mutual Action Plan. The questions you keep to yourself belong in Discovery Call Questions; the agenda is the part the client sees.

## Inputs
- The thread so far, or a summary of how the call was arranged, with date and length
- What you need to learn, and the roles you hope will attend
- Whether anyone on either side wants to record or bring a notetaker
If you have none of this, I start from the date and length and mark the note as a first draft. With the Gmail connector I read the thread; the note is written here and you copy, edit and send it.

## Approach
The up-front contract, as taught by Sandler, agrees five things before a meeting: purpose, their agenda, your agenda, time and outcome. The valuable part is the outcome line. Once "this is not a fit" has been written down as an acceptable ending, both sides can be straight with each other. Recited like a script it reads as a sales technique, so it stays a short, warm note. Recording rules differ by place; the note asks first and defaults to no recording.

## Workflow
1. Ask, in one go: what does the client want from this call, in their words if you have them? Who has said they will attend? Does anyone want to record or use a notetaker?
2. Write the purpose in one sentence tied to their decision and not your pitch: to understand [their situation] well enough to say whether and how you could help.
3. Put their agenda first: what they asked to cover, or an open line inviting them to add to it. Then yours: two or three topics you need to learn about, written as topics, never the questions themselves.
4. State the time, and that you will confirm it at the start in case their day has changed.
5. Name the possible endings and call each one fine: a next step with a date, "not now", or "not a fit", in which case you will say so and point them elsewhere if you can.
6. List who would be useful by role (owns the problem, would run the work, decides) and ask your contact whether they can join. Never insist, never write to them directly. Add one consent sentence: no recording unless everyone agrees at the start, and a question about any notetaker they plan to bring. Recording rules vary by place; check with a qualified adviser. The note should stay short enough to read on a phone.

## Output Format
```markdown
# Call Agenda Note
To: [contact] · Call: [date, time, length] · A draft; you send it
## The note
Subject: [the call and its date]
[Greeting],
Purpose: [one sentence linked to the decision they face]
Your topics: [what they asked to cover, or "anything to add?"]
Mine: [two or three topics] · Time: [length]; I will check it at the start.
How it may end: a next step with a date, "not now", or "not a fit". All three are fine.
Useful to have: [roles], if they can make it. Recording: none unless everyone agrees at the start. [Question about their notetaker.]
[Sign-off]
## Attendees by role
| Role needed | Confirmed | Asked through |
|---|---|---|
| [role] | [yes / no / unknown] | [your contact] |
## Decision
[You] edit and send the note by [date, at least a day ahead]; [contact role] confirms or changes the agenda and the attendees.
```

## Done When
- Purpose, their agenda, your agenda, time and outcomes are all present, with "not a fit" among the outcomes
- Attendees are roles, requested through your contact
- The consent line defaults to no recording, and the note reads like a person wrote it

## Quality Bar
- Their agenda sits above yours, and yours lists topics, never your question page
- No pressure phrases and no "to make the best use of your time" padding
- Attendees are suggested by role, never by a view on an individual
- No recording or notetaker without everyone's consent, asked first
- I draft the note from what you gave me and invent no agreement; you send it and you run the call.
