# Negotiation Plan

## When To Use
The email says "we would love to work with you, can you sharpen the number?" You are a business of your own with a quiet month ahead, and the reflex is to take something off before anyone has to feel awkward. Run this before you reply, to settle in a calm hour where you stop, what you would trade and what you would want back.

## When Not To Use
If you have not yet decided how to charge, begin with Fee Structure Choice. If they are comparing you with a cheaper quote, run Like-for-Like Quote Comparison first so that you negotiate over the same scope.

## Inputs
- Your proposal: scope, fee, timeline, terms
- What the client asked for, in their words where you have them
- What you would really do if this does not go ahead (other work, the time it frees)
If you have none of this, I start from your fee and the client's request, and mark the plan as a first draft with every number left in [brackets].

## Approach
BATNA, the best alternative to a negotiated agreement, is explained by the Program on Negotiation at Harvard Law School (pon.harvard.edu): know what you will actually do with no deal, and your walk-away point follows from it. The same source says to look beyond price to terms and to reassess as facts change. The plan also separates what is fixed for you from what you would trade, so that nothing you care about is handed over by accident. The failure it prevents is a discount volunteered to fill a pause the client was happy to leave.

## Workflow
1. Ask, in one go: what exactly did the client ask for? What will you do if this does not happen? What must not move (a floor, a scope line, a term)?
2. Write your alternative as it really is, not as you hope. Overrating it is the usual mistake; if it is thin, say so and plan with that. Then set your walk-away point yourself. I never suggest a figure.
3. Write the client's side as far as you know it: their alternatives (another adviser, doing it internally, their own AI, doing nothing) and what they said matters. Mark each known, with its source, or assumed.
4. List what you could trade: scope, timing, phasing, payment terms, how much of your own time is involved, length of support. Beside each, what you would ask in return. A concession with nothing back is a gift, and gifts invite the next request.
5. List what you will not give, each with a plain reason you could say aloud.
6. Set the order of concessions, cheapest for you first, in your figures. Write the line you say before any reduction ("what would need to be true for the fee as quoted to work?"), followed by a version that lowers the fee by taking work out, never by doing the same work for less. Recheck the plan if any fact changes before the call.

## Output Format
```markdown
# Negotiation Plan
Engagement: [client role, proposal date] · The client asked for: [their request]
## My alternative and walk-away
- If there is no agreement, I will: [what you would really do]
- Where I walk away: [your figure or term]
## The client's side, as far as I can tell
| Their alternative | Known or assumed | Source |
|---|---|---|
| [another adviser / internally / their own AI / nothing] | [known / assumed] | [where you heard it] |
## What I can trade
| Tradable | What I offer | What I ask in return | Cost to me |
|---|---|---|---|
| [scope / timing / phasing / payment terms] | [offer] | [ask] | [your figure] |
## What I will not give
- [Item, and the plain reason]
## Concession order
1. [Cheapest for you first]
Before any reduction I ask: [your question]
## Decision
[You] set your walk-away and your first concession by [date], before the call with [client contact role]. Contract and payment terms: check with a qualified adviser.
```

## Done When
- The alternative is something you would actually do, written down
- Every tradable has something asked in return
- What you will not give is listed, each with a reason
- No number in the plan came from anyone but you

## Quality Bar
- Nothing aimed at the other person's psychology: no anchoring tricks, false deadlines or pressure
- The fee falls only when scope falls, unless you decide otherwise
- What you know about their side is kept apart from what you assume
- Unsure about a contract or payment term? Check with a qualified adviser
- You set every number; I propose no fee and no discount and invent nothing about the client's position, and you hold the conversation.
