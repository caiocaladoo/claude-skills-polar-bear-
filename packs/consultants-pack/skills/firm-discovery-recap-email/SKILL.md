# Discovery Recap Email

## When To Use
The call went well and your instinct is to start the proposal tonight. Stop one step earlier. A short playback sent the same day lets the client correct you while it is cheap, and gives you a written "yes, that is it" to build on. It answers one question: did I hear you right, and what did I miss?

## When Not To Use
If a proposal is already out and unanswered, use Proposal Follow-Up Emails. If your notes still mix quotes with shorthand, run Discovery Call Notes first, or the playback will put words in their mouth.

## Inputs
- Your call record, or raw notes with the verbatim lines marked
- The thread with the client
- The next step you agreed, with its date
If you have none of this, I start from what you tell me the client said, keep every line as paraphrase with no quotation marks, and mark the email as a first draft. With the Gmail connector I read the thread; I write here and you send.

## Approach
This is active listening and playback as counsellors and interviewers practise it, described generically: reflect back the whole of what was said, content and feeling, without judging it or advising on it. The failure it prevents is a recap that slides into selling. The moment a playback carries your interpretation, a hint of price or a credential, the client stops checking it and starts guarding against it.

## Workflow
1. Ask, in one go: what next step was agreed, and for when? Which two or three things did the client say mattered most, in their order? Was there anything you are not sure you understood?
2. Build "what I heard" from the verbatim bin only. Use short phrases of theirs in quotation marks and put nothing inside or beside a quote that shifts its meaning. Paraphrase stays outside the marks.
3. Write "what matters most to you" in the order they gave, not the order that suits your offer. If they named no order, say so and ask.
4. Write "what I am unsure about" as two or three plain questions from your open list. Reflect a feeling only where they named it themselves ("you said this has worn the team down"), never as your reading of their mood.
5. Add the next step with a date and an owner, then one explicit line inviting correction.
6. Remove anything that sells: price, a preview of the proposal, credentials, cases, advice. It should be readable on a phone in under a minute.

## Output Format
```markdown
# Playback Email Draft
Subject: [What I heard on [date], and one question]
[Name],
Thank you for the time today. Before I write anything further, here is what I heard, so that you can correct it.
## What I heard
- [Point in their words, short quotes marked]
- [Point]
## What matters most to you
1. [Priority, in their order]
2. [Priority]
## What I am unsure about
- [Plain question]
## What happens now
[Step] by [date], [owner].
Did I get anything wrong or leave anything out? One line back is plenty.
[Your name]
## Decision
[You] decide by [the end of the day after the call] whether to send this as written, edit it or phone instead; you send it.
```

## Done When
- Every quotation mark holds words from the verbatim bin
- Priorities follow the client's order and the open points are plain questions
- There is a dated next step and an explicit invitation to correct
- No price, pitch, credential or advice appears anywhere

## Quality Bar
- Reflect, never evaluate; no "great point", no verdict on what they told you
- A feeling is reflected only where the client named it
- Plain words and short sentences, with no jargon the client did not use first
- Their words only, nothing added to a quote and nothing invented; you read it, edit it and send it yourself.
