# Like-for-Like Quote Comparison

## When To Use
The client forwards another firm's quote with one line: "can you get closer to this?" It is a fraction of yours. Before you defend your fee or cut it, find out whether the two documents describe the same work. Run this when you hold the other quote, or enough of it to compare line by line.

## When Not To Use
With only a number and no document, there is nothing to line up; use the Objection Handling Map for the price objection. If the scope is agreed and the argument is about your fee and terms, use Negotiation Plan.

## Inputs
- Your proposal or scope, including deliverables, timeline and terms
- The other quote as the client shared it, or the parts they told you (say which)
- What the client said about why the cheaper quote appeals
If you have none of this beyond your own scope, I build the empty grid from it and mark it as a first draft for when the other quote arrives.

## Approach
Buyers who manage tenders put every quote into the same line items before looking at price, a procurement habit often called bid levelling and described generically here. Once both are on identical lines, the difference usually turns out to be scope, review or risk and not generosity. The failure it prevents is the sour comparison that hints the other firm cuts corners; the client hears wounded pride and stops listening. A lower quote that relies on AI is not a defect in itself.

## Workflow
1. Ask, in one go: did the client share the other quote in order to have it compared? Which version of your scope is it up against? What did the client say matters most?
2. Put both quotes on the same lines: deliverables, acceptance criteria, timeline, rounds of revision, what the client's people must do, who owns the output, support afterwards, price, payment terms. Add any line either quote mentions. Where a quote says nothing, write "not stated" and never a guess.
3. For each line mark what yours includes, what theirs includes, and the risk the client carries if the line is absent, written as a consequence for the client and never as a judgment of the other firm.
4. Examine your own quote as hard as theirs. Where yours is silent or vague, list it; that is yours to fix.
5. Draft neutral questions the client could put to either firm, useful whoever wins: "who checks the output before it reaches us?", "what happens if the first version misses the mark?".
6. Set out your options: narrow your scope to match and reprice it, offer a smaller version, or step aside with a gracious note. You choose, and every figure is yours.

## Output Format
```markdown
# Quote Comparison Grid
Compared: [your proposal, date] and [other quote, as shared by the client, date]
## Line by line
| Line | Your quote | Their quote | Risk the client carries if absent |
|---|---|---|---|
| [deliverable / acceptance / timeline / revisions / ownership / support / price / terms] | [included / not stated / detail] | [included / not stated / detail] | [consequence for the client] |
## Gaps in your own quote
- [A line on which your quote says little or nothing, and how you will fix it]
## Questions the client could put to either firm
1. [neutral question]
## Your options
| Option | What changes | What it costs you |
|---|---|---|
| Match the scope and reprice | [lines removed] | [your figure] |
| A smaller version | [what it contains] | [your figure] |
| Step aside | [the note you would send] | [none] |
## Decision
[You] decide by [date] which option to take, before you reply to [client contact role].
```

## Done When
- Both quotes sit on the same lines, with "not stated" wherever one is silent
- Each risk is a consequence for the client, never a remark about the other firm
- Your own gaps are listed
- The questions serve the client whichever firm they choose

## Quality Bar
- Only what the two documents say, or what the client told you, marked as such
- No guess about the other firm's methods, tools, people or quality
- The other quote is never shared beyond the client who gave it to you
- Facts from both documents only and the rival never disparaged; I invent no line and no price, and you choose how to respond.
