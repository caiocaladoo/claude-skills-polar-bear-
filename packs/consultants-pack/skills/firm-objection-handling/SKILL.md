# Objection Handling Map

## When To Use
"It is more than we budgeted." "Now is not a good moment." "Could you send something over?" You have heard each of them a dozen times and still answer on the spot, then think of the better reply on the way home. Run this before a proposal meeting, to prepare truthful responses to the objections you really get, with the proof you really have.

## When Not To Use
"AI could do this" needs its own task split; use AI Objection Answer. When terms are on the table and a discount is being asked for, use Negotiation Plan.

## Inputs
- The objections you have heard, in the client's words where you have them
- Your proposal or scope, and where this conversation stands
- Your proof: cases you may cite, figures you can show, past clients who agreed to take a call
If you have none of this, I start from four common ones (price, timing, "we will do it in house", "send me something") and mark the map as a first draft.

## Approach
LAER, from Carew International (carew.com), handles an objection in four moves: listen, acknowledge, explore, respond. The skill is in Explore. "Send me something" can mean "I am interested and need to persuade my director" or "I would like this call to end", and the right reply differs. The failure it prevents is the rebuttal sheet, which skips Explore and fires a prepared line at whatever was said.

## Workflow
1. Ask, in one go: which objections come up at this stage? Which one cost you an engagement recently? What proof can you actually show?
2. One row per objection. Under Listen, write what to listen for: the exact words and what follows them. Let the client finish.
3. Under Acknowledge, write one sentence agreeing with what is true in it ("it is a serious sum", "your quarter is already full"), with no "but" attached.
4. Under Explore, write the question that finds the concern beneath. Price: compared with what? Timing: what would need to change for now to work? In house: who would do it, and what would they put down? "Send me something": what would you do with it, and who else would read it? A row with no explore question is a rebuttal.
5. Under Respond, draft an honest reply to each likely answer, in your voice. Where the truthful reply is "then this is not the moment", write that.
6. Fill Proof from your files only: the case, figure or reference behind the response, or "none yet". Then fill "What I will not say": pressure lines, deadlines you would not hold, scarcity you cannot show, claims you cannot prove.

## Output Format
```markdown
# Objection Map
Stage: [where the conversation stands] · Prepared for: [meeting, date]
| Objection | Listen for | Acknowledge | Explore question | Respond | Proof held | What I will not say |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| [price] | [their words] | [what is true] | [question] | [honest reply] | [case / figure / reference, or none yet] | [line to avoid] |
| [timing] | [their words] | [what is true] | [question] | [honest reply] | [proof, or none yet] | [line to avoid] |
| [in house] | [their words] | [what is true] | [question] | [honest reply] | [proof, or none yet] | [line to avoid] |
## Proof gaps
- [Response that needs proof you lack, and how you will obtain it with permission]
## Decision
[You] choose by [date] the responses you will use at [meeting] and the proof gaps to close before it.
```

## Done When
- An explore question sits in every row
- Each acknowledgement names something true and is not followed by "but"
- Every proof cell names a real item from your files or reads "none yet"
- No row has an empty "will not say" cell

## Quality Bar
- A response answers the real concern, not the surface words
- "This is not the right moment for you" is an allowed response
- A past client's results appear only with that client's permission
- No pressure, no deadline you would not keep, no scarcity you cannot show
- Honest responses backed by proof you hold; I invent no case, figure or reference, and you have the conversation yourself.
