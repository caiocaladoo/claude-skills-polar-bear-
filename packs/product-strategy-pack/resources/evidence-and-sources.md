# Evidence and sources

Research checked 11 October 2026.

## What this guide claims

The guide claims that each skill applies one named method or produces one artifact of a product strategy cycle, that a named method is attributed to its originator through a public page, a paper or an institution's guidance, and that each skill says where its method breaks. It claims that the eight stages follow the order in which the work is usually done (diagnosis first, review last, and a loop back to the roadmap), and that the "When to run it" lines come from what product people say they struggle with.

## Methods described without an originator

Some methods are long-standing practice with no single public originator page, or their detailed sources are books. The skills describe them generically and name no author: the decision frame and the rule that options must differ in kind (decision-quality practice), the issue tree whose branches do not overlap and together cover the question, hypothesis-led problem solving, TAM, SAM and SOM with top-down and bottom-up sizing, the weighted decision matrix and the plain consequence table, scenarios as plausible futures with one-at-a-time sensitivity analysis, and leading and lagging indicators with a guardrail.

## Deliberate design choices

These are Polar Bear's own design choices, not findings from the sources. The red line is ours: Claude structures the thinking and drafts the documents, and the strategy is a set of choices a named person makes and can defend. We ask for "confirmed" or "assumed" beside every customer claim, for ranges instead of point estimates, and for kill criteria, weights and review dates written before results arrive. Nothing in the guide scores, grades, ranks or profiles a person, a team or a sponsor: options are scored and key results are graded, and a review names causes in the market, the product or the measurement. Tax, accounting, contract, disclosure and board-duty points are referred onward with "check with a qualified adviser" and are never stated as advice. The guide names the reader's role only, because the methods do not depend on anything else about the reader. The strategy document, the one-page cut and the deck are kept as three artifacts for three readers, and the lean canvas is framed as the business logic of one bet inside the strategy. Where a source was written for another setting, the adaptation is ours. No efficacy is claimed.
