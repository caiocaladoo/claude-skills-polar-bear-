# Evidence and sources

Research checked 11 October 2026.

## What this guide claims

The guide claims that each skill applies its method faithfully, attributes a named method to its originator through a public source, and states where the method breaks. It claims that the seven stages follow the order in which AI adoption work is usually done, and that the "When to run it" lines come from what people who run this work say they struggle with.

## Deliberate design choices

These are Polar Bear's own choices, not findings from the sources above.

- **The red line.** Adoption is something people choose: Claude prepares the plan, the sessions and the evidence, a named person makes every call, nobody's use of AI is tracked, scored or ranked, and no saving is claimed that the organisation's own evidence does not show.
- **No per-person measure of any kind.** No skill reads usage data about individuals. The evidence review works from what the team itself provides, at team level, with counts beside percentages and small groups merged or suppressed. Several sources measure individuals; this guide does not carry that over.
- **Anonymous by default.** The starting point survey is anonymous, with a minimum group size the user sets and a list of what is never asked. Claude does not infer who wrote an answer.
- **Concerns are not called resistance.** The barrier diagnosis checks time, tools, access and permission before motivation, and the concern skills check the substance of a concern, however it arrived, without looking for its author.
- **Volunteers and consent.** A champions network is volunteers with protected time and is never a ranking or a reporting line on colleagues. Use stories are voluntary, told with consent, anonymous, never chosen by ranking people, and each is marked as one case, not a rate.
- **Four judgements instead of a prompt list.** The curriculum, the output check, the use case map and the prompt library are built on four judgements described in our own words: what to hand over, how to ask, how to check, and what you stay responsible for.
- **Method scores are left out.** Where a source offers a scoring system for a change or for contributors, the guide uses the questions and leaves the score.
- **Adviser referrals.** The skills explain rules an organisation already has; they do not write policy or state law. Every legal, data protection or employment point ends with "check with a qualified adviser".
- **Roles only.** The guide names the role of its reader and nothing about company size, sector or type.
